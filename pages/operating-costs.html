The insurance line is the one to fix first

Premiums follow the dwelling-coverage limit, and the limit has to follow rebuilding cost, not market value or an estimate taken from a typical house. The owner's working figures are plausible for an average home in this state and are not numbers to plan a high-value house or a three-unit let building on: two written quotes with the declarations page attached replace the band, and the model reruns. Nothing in this project claims to know a carrier's rate.

Why the utility lines are not footnotes

A house whose electric bill is a credit is worth a few hundred dollars a year more than the same house without the array, and a building where the owner pays water for three units is worth several thousand a year less than one where tenants pay it. Both lines are contractual and local: the export tariff terms for the array, the sewer and storm-water charges, and the tenant-reimbursement arrangements have to be read from the bills and the leases, not from a model.

Why the first year of that table does not equal the records

The starting-year rows are moved one percent away from the record they were seeded from, which is invisible in a decision and decisive in a search box. An escalated, modelled figure is the model's own; a figure that still matches a single bill line or a single roll entry is somebody's record, and it stays in the private workpaper.
