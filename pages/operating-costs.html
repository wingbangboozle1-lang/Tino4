The insurance line is the one to fix first

Premiums follow the dwelling-coverage limit, and the limit has to follow rebuilding cost, not market value or an estimate taken from a typical house. An owner estimate of $1,500 a year is plausible for an average home in this state and is not a number to plan a high-value house on: two written quotes with the declarations page attached replace it, and the model reruns. Nothing in this project claims to know a carrier's rate.

Why the utility lines are not footnotes

A house whose electric bill is a credit is worth a few hundred dollars a year more than the same house without the array, and a building where the owner pays water for three units is worth about five thousand dollars a year less than one where tenants pay it. Both lines are contractual and local: the export tariff terms for the array, the sewer and storm-water charges, and the tenant-reimbursement arrangements have to be read from the bills and the leases, not from a model.
