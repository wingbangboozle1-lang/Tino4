Executive summary

The high-impact unknowns, the verified figures, and a safe order of operations.

Executive takeaway

The ranking below is modelled, not known: it is what the published assumptions produce, and it moves when an assumption is replaced by evidence. Keep the estate-tax, income-tax basis and property-tax assessment questions separate, obtain the written evidence named on the questions page, and agree the decision criteria before treating any table as a conclusion.

How to use this guide

Read in order: start at Decision overview for the first-screen decision-changers, then this summary, then Compare paths for the pure and mixed choices, then Questions & missing evidence for what to obtain and what official sources already answer. Use the stress-test pages (Black-swan conditions, Treasury staging tool, investment policy) once the evidence is in hand. Every table row links to the official source for its narrow rule; rows that cannot be confirmed are left blank and flagged rather than guessed.

Recommendation standard

Do not call a projection the best plan until it includes the same tax year and horizon for all alternatives; the full sale-cost waterfall (transaction costs, documentary transfer tax, recording fees, sale-year income tax including unrecaptured section 1250 gain and the California 1% add-on above $1,000,000 of taxable income); any like-kind-exchange decision; debt; occupancy; rental legality; vacancy; capital costs; utilities; insurance; estate liquidity; and reinvestment risk.

Order of operations

First agree in writing what would make the household choose each path. Second, confirm trust, title, debts and the transfer date. Third, obtain independent valuations and a written date-of-death basis. Fourth, establish the property-tax treatment and the Proposition 19 eligibility facts, including whether long-term occupancy is genuinely intended. Fifth, price recurring costs from quotes, bills and bids. Sixth, compare every pure and mixed path at identical dates and horizons with taxes, debt, occupancy, capital costs and liquidity. Seventh, model the reinvestment decision under uneven regimes. Eighth, record the decision, the date and the rejected alternatives.

What would change the answer

A change in any one of these can reverse the ranking: the transfer date and the law in force then; the date-of-death value; the heir's genuine long-term occupancy intention; eligibility for and the indexed amount of the property-tax exclusion; the enacted local transfer-tax schedule; the sale-year tax computation including depreciation recapture; the market rent for the inherited home; insurance availability and price; the five-year capital plan; estate liquidity and any federal filing; and whether a like-kind exchange is wanted before any listing. Re-run the model whenever one of these changes and record whether the recommendation moved.
