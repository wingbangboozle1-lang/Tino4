EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Executive summary

The short version: keep the already-owned property, sell the inherited house near the transfer and stage the proceeds — unless the household wants to live in the inherited house — with four verification gates and every case figure rounded to $10.

Executive conclusion — one conditional recommendation, four gates

Working answer: keep the already-owned San Francisco property, sell the inherited house soon after the date of death (stepped-up basis keeps income tax minimal), and stage the net proceeds through 24–36 months of 3-month to 2-year Treasuries before phasing into equities — or occupy the inherited house and claim the family-home exclusion if that is the residence the household actually wants. Renting the inherited house without occupying it is the weakest path because reassessment alone costs about $32,550–$41,540 per year before any rent is known. This ranking is conditional on four gates: (1) the trust and title documents confirm the transfer and sale authority; (2) the estate inventory confirms liquidity and any federal estate-tax position; (3) written insurance, rent and repair evidence replaces the owner estimates; (4) the property-tax bills reconcile the implied rates shown here. Reinvestment risk is part of the headline, not a footnote: staging reduces crash-during-wait exposure but exposes maturing cash to future auction yields.
