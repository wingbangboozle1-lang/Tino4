Recommendation standard

Do not call a projection the best plan until it includes the same tax year and horizon for all alternatives; the full sale-cost waterfall (transaction costs, documentary transfer tax, recording fees, sale-year income tax including unrecaptured section 1250 gain and the California 1% add-on above $1,000,000 of taxable income); any like-kind-exchange decision; debt; occupancy; rental legality; vacancy; capital costs; utilities; insurance; estate liquidity; and reinvestment risk.

The answer, with its reasons attached

On the unit-dollar inputs published here, selling the inherited home in the transfer year and investing the proceeds ranks ahead of every hold path at both the ten-year and the twenty-five-year horizon, in all three growth scenarios. The reasons are checkable rather than rhetorical. The transfer moves the annual property tax from $2,400 to $18,030 with a valid exclusion claim, or $23,660 without one. A let home on these assumptions nets about $18,250 a year before income tax on a modeled value of $1,753,510. A transfer-year sale is tax-light only because the modeled contract price equals the stepped-up basis, so a gap between the two is taxed in the sale year. Choosing to hold is a bet that the home grows faster than 5.04% a year; choosing to live in it is a bet that occupying the house is worth more than the $5,430 a year the model shows it costs, against letting the third unit of the building the heir already owns.

The one tested switch that reverses the answer

Set the portfolio assumption to the soft scenario - 3.0% a year before a 0.3% tax drag, which is less than the modeled Treasury reserve pays while money waits - and the ranking turns: Keep it, rent it out, sell at the horizon finishes about $189,700 ahead at the twenty-five-year horizon, and the ten-year leader becomes Sell, park 30 months in Treasuries, then buy. That is not a forecast of a weak market; it is the one published condition under which this model stops recommending a sale, and the reason the investment policy is written as a rule with a review date before any money moves.

What this answer is not

It is not advice, not a valuation and not a forecast. It is the arithmetic the published assumptions produce, shown so the household can see precisely which assumption it would have to accept - or replace - to change its mind. Model figures computed 2026-11-17 from labelled assumptions and the household's report, restated in unit dollars so no household figure is published; re-run with the owner's written records before relying on any of them.
