Executive summary

A trust is expected to transfer Property A (Cupertino 95014) plus a cash amount [CASH AMOUNT] to [BENEFICIARY] sometime in 2040–2050. Property B (San Francisco 94122) is the household's residence: two of its three units are rented and the household can live there without giving up rental income (user-supplied, unverified). Because housing is already available at no incremental cost, occupying Property A produces no financial benefit in this model while adding substantial carrying costs.

The single largest financial event at inheritance is property tax reassessment. California reassesses trust-held real property to market value as of the date of death, not on distribution. Using a 3% annual appreciation assumption, Property A's assessed value in 2045 would move from about $802,030 (Prop 13 capped) to $5,435,870. Annual property tax at the County's published typical rate rises from about $9,210 to $62,450 — a jump of $53,230 per year. The Prop 19 parent-child exclusion, if all of its conditions are met, would reduce that to about $50,370 per year (saving roughly $12,000/yr), but it cannot eliminate the reassessment because the published allowance is capped at the factored base year value plus $1,044,590.

Three options were modelled over 10 years from each inheritance year. Property taxes, insurance, water, electricity, maintenance, selling costs and federal capital-gains/NIIT tax are included; ordinary income tax on rents is deliberately excluded, which is generous to the rent option. The sell option uses the verified step-up-in-basis rule, so a prompt sale creates essentially no capital gain.

Best plan: sell and reinvest

Selling promptly after inheritance (a) uses the stepped-up basis to eliminate capital gains tax, (b) avoids an annual reassessment-driven property tax increase of about $53,230, (c) avoids insurance, utilities and maintenance on a second home, and (d) converts an illiquid concentrated asset into a diversified portfolio. The model's break-even equity return for 2045 is about 3.9% — below that, renting could be competitive. Confirm the Prop 19 principal-residence question and the actual tax bill before acting.
