Executive summary

The short version: separate the assets, verify the rules that can change the answer, and leave private numbers blank until documents establish them.

Executive conclusion

No defensible best path can be selected yet. First resolve the legal transfer and tax facts; then build after-tax cash-flow cases for Property A and Property B separately; then compare pure and mixed choices under adverse market, inflation, repair and insurance conditions.

What this workspace concludes, and what it refuses to conclude

Three things are currently established well enough to rely on: the federal estate-tax threshold is an estate-level test that includes lifetime taxable gifts, California does not impose its own death-transfer tax under current law, and the intergenerational property-tax exclusion is conditional and claim-driven. Three things are not established: the trust's actual terms and authority, the household's estate composition and basis, and every property-level cost, rent and value. A best path is therefore not published. What is published is the exact sequence that would produce one: settle the legal and tax facts, price the carrying costs from real documents, then compare pure and mixed paths under stated ranges and adverse regimes.

What would change the conclusion fastest

If counsel confirms an unconstrained sale authority and the estate is comfortably below the filing threshold, the decision collapses to a comparison of after-tax proceeds against the net cash flow from holding. If the trust restricts sale or imposes occupancy conditions, most of the reinvestment analysis becomes moot until those conditions are resolved. If a family-home exclusion is unavailable, the property-tax difference alone can dominate every market scenario on this site.
