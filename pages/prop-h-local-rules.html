The passed San Francisco Measure H parcel tax with its official terms, and Property A's Santa Clara County tax rate area from the official Compilation of Tax Rates.

A passed San Francisco parcel tax and a Santa Clara rate area

Measure H is a passed San Francisco parcel tax that adds a flat, building-area-based charge to Property B for 15 years from July 1, 2027. Property A sits in Santa Clara County, where its property tax is set by its own tax rate area in the County Controller-Treasurer's Compilation of Tax Rates. These are local, parcel-specific figures that must be read from the official records for each property.

Measure H — title and statusMeasure H, 'Parcel Tax to Fund Muni Operations', appeared on the San Francisco November 3, 2026 ballot and passed with 74.4% YES (preliminary, official Department of Elections results).A passed local measure, not a proposal; confirm the operative ordinance, implementation and any tenant pass-through rules before applying it.VerifiedSan Francisco Department of Elections — Measure H results (Nov 3, 2026)
What it taxesA new annual real-estate parcel tax on parcels of real property in San Francisco, at rates ranging from $129 per parcel up to a maximum of $400,000 per parcel by property type and size, adjusted annually for inflation.The tax is a flat parcel charge based on building area, not a percentage of value; it is separate from the property-tax roll.VerifiedSan Francisco Department of Elections — Measure H results (Nov 3, 2026)
Duration and useThe tax runs from July 1, 2027 to June 30, 2042 (15 years) and dedicates revenue to Muni (SFMTA) operations.The measure covers the planning window; confirm the exact operative dates and any extension or repeal in the ordinance.VerifiedSan Francisco Department of Elections — Measure H results (Nov 3, 2026)
Single-family residential parcelsBase tax of $129 for the first 3,000 square feet of building area, plus 42 cents per square foot for area between 3,001 and 5,000 square feet and 199 cents per square foot above 5,000 square feet.This is the official rate schedule for single-family parcels; it does not apply to a multifamily parcel such as Property B.VerifiedSan Francisco Department of Elections — Measure H results (Nov 3, 2026)
Multifamily residential parcelsBase tax of $249 for the first 5,000 square feet of building area, plus 19.5 cents per square foot of building area over 5,000 square feet, capped at $50,000 per parcel.Property B is a three-unit building; its Measure H tax depends on its total building area, which is not published here.VerifiedSan Francisco Department of Elections — Measure H results (Nov 3, 2026)
Non-residential and mixed-use parcelsNon-residential parcels carry a base tax of $799 for the first 5,000 square feet plus marginal rates by size, capped at $400,000 per parcel; mixed-use parcels carry a $799 base capped at $400,000.Confirm the parcel's classification and the applicable marginal rates from the operative ordinance.VerifiedSan Francisco Department of Elections — Measure H results (Nov 3, 2026)
Exemptions and pass-throughMost properties owned and occupied as a primary residence by a person at least 65 years old are exempt or reduced; single-room-occupancy building area is excluded. Owners of rent-controlled residential units may pass through up to 50% of the tax to tenants, capped at $65 per unit, if the initial base rent was set before June 1, 2027.Exemption eligibility and the pass-through cap depend on the unit's rent-control status and the base rent date; confirm with the City.VerifiedSan Francisco Department of Elections — Measure H results (Nov 3, 2026)

How to read the local layer

Measure H and the Santa Clara tax rate area are additive local costs, not a single rate. For Property B, obtain the building area and the operative Measure H ordinance to compute the exact parcel charge. For Property A, read the parcel's TRA block from the County Controller-Treasurer's Compilation of Tax Rates to get the total ad-valorem rate, then apply it to the carried-over or reassessed base on the projections page. Both figures re-check annually.
