Estate & inheritance tax

What the thresholds actually are at each level, and how much cash would push the estate over the federal line.

There is no inheritance tax to pay

This surprises people, so it is worth being blunt. California has no inheritance tax and no state estate tax California State Controller's Office. Inherited cash and property are not taxable income when received, federally or in California California Franchise Tax Board. Neither Santa Clara County nor San Francisco levies anything on an inheritance; the only charge at the county level is the recording fee and, on a sale for consideration, the documentary transfer tax.

The tax that exists is the federal estate tax, it is paid by the estate rather than the heir, and it applies only to the amount above $15,000,000 per decedent in 2026 Internal Revenue Service. A married couple can effectively shelter twice that with a portability election on a timely Form 706.

So the real question is whether the estate crosses the threshold

The three thresholds, in plain language

LevelThresholdWhat happens at itSource
Local (Santa Clara County / San Francisco)NoneNo inheritance or estate tax at either county or city level. Recording fees and, on a sale, documentary transfer tax only.Santa Clara County Office of the Clerk-Recorder
CaliforniaNoneNo California estate tax return required for any death on or after January 1, 2005. No inheritance tax. No state GST tax after 2004. The heir owes nothing to the state for inheriting.California State Controller's Office
Federal$15,000,000 per decedent (2026)Tax is on the estate, above the exclusion, at a 40% top rate. Portability lets a surviving spouse use the first spouse's unused exclusion. Gifts above $19,000 per recipient per year count against the lifetime exclusion.Internal Revenue Service

How to stay under it, in order of effectiveness

  1. Use both spouses' exclusions. A portability election on a timely Form 706 for the first spouse to die can roughly double the amount sheltered, for the cost of filing a return Internal Revenue Service. This is the single largest lever and it is often missed.
  2. Use the annual gift exclusion while it lasts. $19,000 per recipient per donor in 2026, or $38,000 for a couple splitting gifts, with no lifetime-exclusion cost and no return required if no other gifts were made Internal Revenue Service. The trade-off: a gifted asset loses the step-up in basis, so gifting low-basis property can create a larger income-tax problem than the estate tax it avoids.
  3. Do not gift the real property. Gifting Property A during life forfeits the date-of-death basis step-up under IRC §1014 Internal Revenue Service and forfeits the Prop 19 parent-child exclusion, which only applies to a transfer at death. On these numbers that is a bad trade.
  4. Confirm what is actually in the estate. Life insurance proceeds, retirement accounts and jointly held property all count. The two properties are only part of the gross estate.

What is not modelled here

  • The actual cash amount. Withheld by policy. The headroom column is the answer to "how much is too much" without ever writing the number down.
  • Forward indexation of the exclusion. The IRS has not published amounts after 2026, so the model uses the 2026 figure as a floor. Flagged F11.
  • Proposed California estate tax. Several bills have been floated and none is enacted. Listed on the Pending legislation page and used nowhere in this table.
  • Valuation discounts, charitable deductions, and trust structures. All real levers, all dependent on facts not supplied, and none of them modelled.
