Sale & income-tax layer

Documentary transfer tax, recording fees, the Section 121 exclusion, depreciation and recapture, and the California income-tax layer on a sale.

Three separate tax systems, one sale

A sale of Property A touches three distinct calculations at once: the county documentary transfer tax and recording fees (a transaction cost), the federal/California income tax on any gain (basis-driven, with the Section 121 exclusion only if the home was the owner's principal residence for two of five years), and the California 1% surcharge above $1,000,000 of taxable income. Property-tax reassessment is a fourth, separate system. Do not collapse them into one 'tax' number.

Sale-tax what-if — local only

Enter the gross sale price, the income-tax basis, whether the Section 121 use test is met, and the household's California taxable income. Same-tax-year sale, before anything else

If the household is considering a same-year sale, run the income-tax comparison first: with an inherited date-of-death basis, a same-year sale near the valuation date may produce little gain and no Section 121 shelter, while a living-then-sell path preserves the exclusion but delays liquidity. The documentary transfer tax and recording fees apply to either sale. Confirm the actual basis, sale price and California taxable income with a CPA before choosing.
