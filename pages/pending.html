What is being discussed, what it would change, and why none of it touches a single computed number.

Nothing on this page is in any projection

The request asked to look at changes in the law currently being discussed and how they would affect the decision. This page is that, kept strictly separate. A projection that mixes enacted and proposed law is not a projection — it is a guess with a decimal point. Every number elsewhere on this site uses only enacted law.

The two that actually matter here

AB 1482 disappears on January 1, 2030 — as written

Civil Code §1947.12 says, in terms, that the section "shall remain in effect until January 1, 2030, and as of that date is repealed" California Legislative Information. That is not a proposal; it is the current statute. What is unknown is whether the Legislature extends it.

Why it matters: if the cap lapses, rent on a rented-out Property A could reset to market. If it is extended or tightened, the rent-out case gets worse. The model uses a flat 2%/yr across the entire horizon precisely so it does not quietly assume either outcome. Flagged F17.

San Francisco may cap banked increases

Board of Supervisors File No. 261020, posted November 9 2026, would temporarily limit combined banked and capital-improvement increases on rent-controlled units to 10% a year City and County of San Francisco. The Rent Board's own page states clearly that it "has not been enacted and is subject to change."

Why it matters: it caps upside on Property B's two rented units. Because Property B is held in all three strategies, a change there moves all three terminal values roughly together and is unlikely to change which strategy wins — but it would lower every number on the site.

Also worth watching, but unsourced and therefore excluded

Recurring California proposals for a state estate tax and for annual wealth taxes were considered. In this research pass no enacted law and no confirmable pending bill text was found to cite, so they are listed on the Flagged page as unverified rather than described here as if they were concrete. If they ever pass, they would add a state-level layer on top of the federal one and would change the estate analysis — but nothing on this site pretends to predict that.
