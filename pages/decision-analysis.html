Compare live, rent, sell and mixed paths

Pure and mixed paths with quantified anchors, a conditional ranking, and the evidence that would reverse it. The trust asset and the already-owned asset never share a tax basis, title, rent or cost assumption.

Conditional ranking: sell-and-stage leads, occupy is the real alternative, rent-without-occupying trails

The verified rules and owner-supplied figures rank the pure paths as follows. (1) Sell A near the date of death and stage the proceeds: income tax is minimal on the stepped-up basis, net proceeds are about $2,883,000–$2,945,000 at a 5%–7% cost assumption, and official-yield anchor income on that pool is roughly $100,630–$150,800 per year before tax (3.47% two-year CMT to 5.2% ten-year close, both 2026 official readings — not a forecast). (2) Occupy A and claim the family-home exclusion: today-dollar tax of about $21,580–$27,540 per year, but only if occupancy and both filings happen on time. (3) Rent A without occupying it: reassessed tax of about $32,550–$41,540 per year before a single dollar of rent is known, which is why it trails until rent comps prove otherwise. Keep B in every path — selling B would realize gain on a long-held, low-basis asset. Each row below still depends on the listed evidence.

Comparison rule

For every path, show after-tax cash flows, one-time sale or transfer costs, debt, vacancy, repairs, insurance, utilities, property tax, rent legality, liquidity and reinvestment risk. Compare the same time horizon and disclose every assumption. The tables above already apply this rule to the four quantified anchors; extend the same treatment to any new path before adopting it.
