Compare live, rent, sell and mixed paths

The comparison is deliberately conditional. It does not assume that the trust asset and the already-owned asset have the same tax basis, title, rent, occupancy or costs.

Conditional ranking under the baseline assumptions (see the stated limits)

Baseline: 2045 transfer, owner-supplied rounded figures, 4% market-growth scenario, 2% assessed-value cap, derived effective rates, 3% exclusion indexing, 7% assumed sale costs, 35% assumed rent load. Under those assumptions the property-tax lever dominates, though the value limit caps how much of it is relief: exclusion-claimed A costs about $62,040 per year at 2045 versus about $87,250 reassessed, and B's $7,150 per month of gross rent continues under every path. Ranking: 1) live in A with the family-home exclusion claimed and keep B rented; 2) rent A only if written rent comparables exceed the derived hurdle (about $3,540 per month gross); 3) sell A (about $6,074,050 net after assumed costs) and stage the proceeds — competitive only after counting where the household lives and the reinvestment risks on the black-swan page. The ranking flips if the exclusion fails, if rent comps beat the hurdle, or if the household's housing cost in the sell path is small. Reinvestment risk belongs beside headline 3: staged Treasuries earn an unknown renewal rate each rung, and staged buying lags a rising market.

Comparison rule

For every path, show after-tax cash flows, one-time sale or transfer costs, debt, vacancy, repairs, insurance, utilities, property tax, rent legality, liquidity and reinvestment risk. Compare the same time horizon and disclose every assumption.
