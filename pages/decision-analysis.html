Compare paths

A fair comparison includes pure and mixed choices, on the same timeline, with rounded figures.

This page does not pick a path, and that is deliberate

Six competitive paths exist: occupy, rent out, sell in the transfer year, occupy then sell, a mixed structure, and sell then reinvest. A defensible ranking requires the same transfer date, the same valuation date, the same after-tax horizon, and evidence for every input each path depends on. Where that evidence is missing the path is marked blocked rather than estimated. The break-even page turns the largest of those unknowns into a single number you can go and obtain.

Model first: a sale in the transfer tax year

Model a sale in the inheritance tax year first, not as a tax-free assumption, but as the path whose basis, costs and tax year must be fixed first. At base values a 2045 transfer-year sale prices about $5,435,870, pays about $271,790 of sale costs, about $5,980 of county documentary transfer tax and roughly $260 of recording fees, leaving about $5,157,840 to invest. Verify the appraisal, amount realized and tax-year rules with the CPA.

Comparison rule

Use the same transfer date, valuation date, after-tax horizon, inflation basis, and household housing need for every path. Show annual cash-flow ranges as well as terminal wealth, and label assumptions rather than presenting them as facts.

Write the rules before the results

Before any forecast arrives, document what would make the household choose each path: minimum liquidity, maximum acceptable maintenance risk, desired occupancy, rental obligations, tax eligibility, acceptable drawdown, and conditions that trigger a sale. The signed version lives on the Decision log page.
