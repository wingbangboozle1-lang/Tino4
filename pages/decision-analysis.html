Inherited basis and the sale-year questionFor property acquired from a decedent, basis is generally the fair market value at the date of death, so a sale in that same year can produce little or no gain to tax. An estate that must file a basis-reporting return can bind the beneficiary's initial basis to the reported estate value.Have the CPA compare a same-year sale with a later sale using the appraisal and the estate's reported values; do not assume both a stepped-up income-tax basis and a low assessed value survive without checking the exclusion conditions.Verified ruleIRS — Gifts and inheritances FAQ · IRS Publication 551 — Basis of Assets · IRS — About Form 8971 (basis reporting)

Comparison rule

Use the same transfer date, valuation date, after-tax horizon, inflation basis, and household housing need for every path. Show annual cash-flow ranges as well as terminal wealth, and label assumptions rather than presenting them as facts.

Write the rules before the results

Before any forecast arrives, document what would make the household choose each path: minimum liquidity, maximum acceptable maintenance risk, desired occupancy, rental obligations, tax eligibility, acceptable drawdown, and conditions that trigger a sale.
