TAXES AND COSTS

Sale, transfer tax and recording fees

The public charges between a sale price and investable cash, priced from the county and city schedules fetched 2026-11-15, plus the income-tax layer that decides what the household keeps.

What this page prices

Selling the inherited house is not just a commission. Two public charges sit between the price and the money that can be invested: a documentary transfer tax collected at recording, and the recorder's fees. This page reads the county and city schedules directly, prices them on the modeled 2045 transfer, and then adds the income-tax layer that decides how much of the net is actually kept. Every rate below comes from the linked official page or statute, fetched 2026-11-15; every household amount is derived and privacy-rounded to the nearest ten dollars.

Transfer tax and recording fees — the published rules

Rate figures are quoted as published and are deliberately not restated in dollars-with-cents form; only amounts derived from the owner's rounded figures are shown as rounded totals.

County authority and base rateRevenue and Taxation Code §11911(a) lets a county impose, on each deed or other instrument conveying real property, a tax of 55 cents for each 500 dollars (or fractional part) of the consideration or value conveyed, when that value exceeds 100 dollars.Santa Clara County's published schedule is exactly that base rate: 0.55 for each 500 dollars of consideration or value, computed on equity or full value of the transfer. Who computes and how it is shownSection 11932 requires every document subject to the tax, in a taxing county, to show on its face the amount of tax due and whether the land is in an incorporated or unincorporated area. Exemptions are itemised by sectionThe Act's exemptions chapter runs from §11921 to §11930.5. Read this session: §11921 exempts an instrument given to secure a debt; §11927 exempts a deed dividing community property under a dissolution judgment or written agreement, provided the deed recites the claim.Nothing read this session exempts a distribution from a revocable trust to a beneficiary. A sale to a third party is a taxable conveyance. Do not assume the trust distribution itself is free of the tax: ask counsel to identify the specific section, and file the declaration if one applies.GuidanceCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §11921
Local voter measures, by citySan Jose's Measure E, collected by the county, applies to transfers over 2,300,000 dollars at tiers of 0.75%, 1% and 1.5% on the entire consideration; Mountain View's Measure G adds 15 dollars per 1,000 dollars on transfers of 6,000,000 dollars or more. Both pages are explicit that they are measures of those cities.Neither applies to a Cupertino parcel. They matter only if the household later sells inside those cities, where a 10,000,000 dollar price can carry a 150,000 dollar city tax.VerifiedSanta Clara County Clerk-Recorder — Measure E (City of San Jose only) · Santa Clara County Clerk-Recorder — Measure G (City of Mountain View only)
Recording fees, Santa Clara CountyThe published schedule charges 102 dollars for the first page of a standard-size document, an amount itemised on the companion page as a 25 dollar base (which includes the 10 dollar real-estate fraud fee, reduced where the document is subject to documentary transfer tax), plus a 75 dollar per-title Building Homes and Jobs Act fee and a 2 dollar restrictive-covenant fee; additional pages are 3 dollars, a conformed copy 5 dollars, and a missing or incomplete Preliminary Change of Ownership Report adds 20 dollars. A 10 dollar survey-monument fee applies to grant deeds without a lot and tract description.A single-family grant deed therefore records for a little over 100 dollars before the transfer tax. The fee is trivial next to the tax but the PCOR is not: an omitted PCOR is what triggers assessor follow-up on a change of ownership.VerifiedSanta Clara County Clerk-Recorder — Record fees and fee schedule · Santa Clara County Clerk-Recorder — Recording real estate (documentary transfer tax)
Recording fees, San Francisco (for the already-owned property)The city-county schedule charges a 14 dollar base for the first page (17 dollars for documents that carry the real-estate fraud fee), 3 dollars per additional page, and a Building Homes and Jobs fee of 75 dollars per parcel per transaction capped at 225 dollars — but the same page lists an exemption for documents transferring real property where transfer tax is paid. A 10 dollar monument fee applies to deeds subject to transfer tax, and 20 dollars is charged when a PCOR is not presented. A transfer-tax affidavit must accompany every document where tax is due or an exemption is claimed.Recorded here because Property B sits in San Francisco: if it is ever sold or refinanced, the affidavit and the PCOR are the two items an owner controls directly.VerifiedSan Francisco Assessor-Recorder — Fees to record a document in San Francisco
San Francisco transfer tax is a different animalThe city-county tax is tiered on the entire consideration: 2.50 dollars per 500 dollars up to 250,000; 3.40 up to 1,000,000; 3.75 from 1,000,000 to under 5,000,000; 11.25 from 5,000,000 to under 10,000,000; 27.50 from 10,000,000 to under 25,000,000; and 30.00 above that, each for every 500 dollars or portion.On the owner-supplied rounded 2026 value of $1,800,000 for Property B, the 3.75 tier alone is about $13,500 — roughly seven times the Santa Clara County cost on the same price. That asymmetry is itself a reason to price a sale of A before contemplating a sale of B.VerifiedSan Francisco Assessor-Recorder — Fees to record a document in San Francisco · Owner-supplied records — Property B (privacy-rounded to nearest $10)

Income-tax layer on a sale of the inherited house

Nothing on this page is a return preparation. The gain figures depend on the estate's actual basis statement, the sale date, the household's other income and the law in the year of death.

California rate treatmentCalifornia has no lower rate for capital gains: all capital gains are taxed as ordinary income, reported on federal Schedule D with California Schedule D (540) where the federal and state treatment of the gain differs.A sale of the inherited house cannot be modeled with a preferential state rate. Where the household has other California income, the gain stacks on top of it.VerifiedFTB — Capital gains and losses
The 1% tax above 1,000,000 dollarsForm 540, line 62, Behavioral Health Services Tax: where taxable income on line 19 exceeds 1,000,000 dollars, the tax is one percent of the excess, computed in whole dollars.Sensitivity value updated: a seven-figure realized gain can push California taxable income past the line-62 threshold, so the state cost of a large gain is the ordinary rate plus this 1%, not the headline bracket alone.VerifiedFTB — 2025 Instructions for Form 540, line 62

Sale in the same tax year as the inheritanceInherited basis is generally the fair market value at the date of death (or the alternate valuation date if the estate elects it), and the estate-tax value reported on a required Schedule A (Form 8971) is the value a beneficiary must generally use.A sale at or near the transfer value therefore produces little or no gain: on the modeled figures the taxable gain is nil,, which is why the property-tax and transfer-cost layers, not income tax, decide the sell path. Waiting for appreciation converts the same sale into a taxed gain.VerifiedIRS Publication 551 (12/2025) — Basis of Assets · IRS — Gifts and inheritances FAQ
Selling after living there — the main-home exclusionUp to 250,000 dollars of gain (500,000 joint) can be excluded where the owner met both the ownership and the use test — 24 months of the 60 months ending on the sale date — and has not excluded gain on another home in the prior two years.Switched on for the live-then-sell paths: two years of occupation is enough to move a further slice of gain out of the tax base. The exclusion is per owner and per sale date, so it must be tested in the year of sale, not the year of transfer.VerifiedIRS Topic no. 701 — Sale of your home
Like-kind exchange — decide before listingSince 2018 only real property qualifies, and only where both the property given up and the property received are held for business or for investment; the replacement property generally takes a carryover basis, and exchange expenses are capitalised into it.A house held to live in is not held for investment, so the plan must be fixed before the sale: exchange into a rental, or sell and pay the tax. Deferral is not avoidance — the carryover basis moves the recapture and the gain to the next sale, and the timing rules in Publication 544 must be read with counsel first.GuidanceIRS Publication 551 (12/2025) — Like-kind exchanges of real property · IRS Publication 544 (2025) — Sales of assets, like-kind exchanges

Decision gates this page exists to serve

Three questions must be answered before a listing, in writing: (1) is a like-kind exchange wanted, since it changes the holding intent the buyer's escrow will document; (2) does the household want the sale in the same tax year as the death, when the gain is smallest; and (3) who pays the transfer tax and the documentary stamps in this county — the customary allocation is negotiable and the amount is public on the recorded document.

Reinvestment risk beside the net-proceeds headline

Everything the sell path produces is the $6,066,670 line above, and the phased-investing plan starts from it. Parking it in 3-month-to-2-year Treasuries for 24-36 months changes when the money faces market risk, not how much there is: each rung reinvests at whatever the next auction pays, staged buying lags a rising market, and the tax on any deferral does not shrink while the cash sits there.
