Briefing sheet for the attorney and the tax adviser

Prepared from owner-supplied figures rounded to the nearest ten dollars, and from statutory and official sources read on 2026-11-15. Not legal, tax or investment advice. Every question below needs a professional answer before any action.

1. The two assets

  • Property A. Anticipated transfer through a revocable trust, Santa Clara County, California, Postal area 95014 (Cupertino). Not owned by the client today. Owner's estimate of value about $3,100,000 as of November 2026. Expected transfer window 2040 to 2050.
  • Property B. Already owned, City and County of San Francisco, Postal area 94122 (Outer Sunset). Three units, two currently let, about $7,150 a month gross on the owner's figures. Owner's estimate of value about $1,800,000.

2. The number that drives everything

  • Modelled value of Property A at the 2045 default transfer year: about $5,959,750.
  • Enrolled assessment at that year: about $802,030.
  • New taxable value if the intergenerational exclusion applies: about $4,407,540. If it does not: about $5,959,750.
  • Annual property tax: about $7,360 today, about $59,560 if occupied, about $80,660 if rented. A multiple of about 8.1x or 11.0x.

3. Questions we need answered in writing

  1. Was the home the transferor's principal residence at transfer, and can evidence of that be produced?
  2. Can the transferee occupy it as a principal residence within one year, and file the homeowners' exemption within one year?
  3. Does the trust permit occupancy, letting or sale, and who must consent?
  4. Confirm the statutory consequence if the exclusion later stops applying: is the base year value established at the change in ownership date, adjusted annually, the value that is enrolled?
  5. What is the gross estate, including adjusted taxable gifts, and is a federal estate return required?
  6. What is the date-of-death valuation, and was an alternate valuation date elected on a filed return?
  7. Was a Form 8971 Schedule A issued, and does a consistent-basis requirement apply?
  8. What is the tax-rate-area rate, and why does Property B's bill imply about 2.136% when the city describes 1 percent plus voter-approved debt and direct assessments?
  9. What local transfer charges would apply on a sale?
  10. What insurance is actually available, for owner-occupied and for landlord use, and at what price?

4. Deadlines we are tracking

  • Trustee notification within 60 days after the trust becomes irrevocable by reason of death.
  • Change-in-ownership statement within 150 days after death where the transfer is outside probate, including through a trust.
  • Federal estate return generally due nine months after death; a six-month extension must be requested before the due date and the estimated tax paid before it.
  • Occupancy as principal residence within one year; homeowners' exemption filed within one year.
  • Exclusion claim within three years, or before transfer to a third party, or before occupancy ends, whichever is earliest.
  • January 1 lien date; February 15 exemption deadline; December 10 and April 10 installment delinquency dates; July 2 to September 15, or November 30, appeal window.

5. Modelling notes the adviser should challenge

  • The effective tax rates are derived from the client's own bills, not from an official county rate. Property A about 1.353%, Property B about 2.136%.
  • Upkeep is modelled as a share of value (0.5 percent for A, 1.0 percent for B). That is a placeholder, not an estimate.
  • Insurance is the client's own estimate of about $1,500 a year for each property and has not been quoted.
  • Selling costs are assumed at 6.0 percent of price. Local transfer charges are not verified.
  • Market rent for Property A is unknown. The break-even gross rent before any financing is about $131,810 a year. Please compare that with three written rent opinions.
  • The state and local tax deduction is capped at $40,000, or $20,000 if married filing separately, subject to a modified adjusted gross income limitation but not reduced below $10,000.
  • The Proposition 19 adjusted amount is $1,044,590 for transfers February 16, 2025 through February 15, 2027, and is recalculated every other February.

6. What would change our recommendation

  • The home is not the transferor's principal residence at transfer.
  • Occupancy within one year is not realistic.
  • The trust restricts occupancy, letting or sale.
  • Insurance is unavailable or unaffordable.
  • Market rent cannot clear the break-even.
  • The estate crosses the filing threshold and liquidity is needed to pay tax.
  • The household needs the proceeds inside the staging window.
