Asset boundary

Asset A is an anticipated trust transfer; Asset B is already owned and may have rent or an occupancy role. Keep each asset's title, basis, assessment, income and costs separate; choose no path until records are verified.

The number that drives everything

Modelled value of Property A at the 2045 default transfer year: about $5,959,750. Enrolled assessment at that year: about $802,030. New taxable value if the intergenerational exclusion applies: about $4,407,550. If it does not: about $5,959,750. Annual property tax: about $7,360 today, about $59,560 if occupied, about $80,660 if rented. A multiple of about 8.1x or 11.0x.

Modelling notes the adviser should challenge

The effective tax rates are derived from the client's own bills, not from an official county rate. Property A about 1.353%, Property B about 2.136%. Upkeep is modelled as a share of value (0.5% for A, 1.0% for B). That is a placeholder, not an estimate. Insurance is the client's own estimate of about $1,500 a year for each property and has not been quoted. Selling costs are assumed at 6.0% of price. Local transfer charges are not verified. Market rent for Property A is unknown. The break-even gross rent before any financing is about $131,810 a year. The state and local tax deduction is capped at $40,000, or $20,000 if married filing separately, subject to a modified adjusted gross income limitation but not reduced below $10,000. The adjusted value limit is $1,044,586 for transfers February 16, 2025 through February 15, 2027, and is recalculated every other February.

What would change our recommendation

The home is not the transferor's principal residence at transfer. Occupancy within one year is not realistic. The trust restricts occupancy, letting or sale. Insurance is unavailable or unaffordable. Market rent cannot clear the break-even of about $131,810 a year. The estate crosses the filing threshold and liquidity is needed to pay tax. The household needs the proceeds inside the staging window.
