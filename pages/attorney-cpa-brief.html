Attorney & CPA Briefing Sheet

Purpose: prepare a decision after an expected trust inheritance. Property A is the future inherited home; Property B is already owned by the inheritor and has existing rental activity. Do not treat A and B as one tax or cash-flow event.

No final recommendation is supportable yet. The leading paths to test are (1) occupy A if the trust permits and Proposition 19 requirements are met, (2) sell A and reinvest after tax, including 24–36 months of Treasury parking/phased equity entry, or (3) rent A only if verified net rental cash flow beats the alternatives. Keep B as a separate baseline.

Questions for counsel

  1. Who is trustee; what powers, distribution terms, debts, title, co-beneficiaries or sale requirements apply?
  2. What is the date-of-death value, basis, trust tax classification and distribution date?
  3. Is the transferee eligible for the family-home exclusion, what occupancy and claim deadlines apply, and what happens if the home is later rented or vacated?
  4. What recorded liens, leases, permits, unit legality, local rent controls, transfer taxes and required notices affect either property?
  5. Which deadlines apply to notice, title, tax returns, change-in-ownership statements and exclusion claims?

Questions for the CPA

  1. Estimate the complete gross estate, prior taxable gifts, marital deductions, portability and federal estate-tax exposure in the year of death; do not compare one house alone to the exclusion.
  2. Confirm federal and California basis, depreciation, capital-gain, NIIT, rental-income and property-tax treatment for each path.
  3. Compare occupancy, rent, immediate sale, and phased reinvestment after taxes with sensitivity ranges—not one point forecast.
  4. For investing, model 3-month to 2-year Treasury reinvestment risk, tax on interest, stock drawdowns, staged-entry dates, liquidity and target allocation.

Documents / facts still needed

Trust and amendments; deed and title report; current tax bills and assessments; date-of-death appraisal; mortgage / lien payoff; insurance declarations and three quotes; utility and repair records; lease and rent roll; rental permits and notices; solar interconnection / tariff; full estate inventory and gift history; intended residence and liquidity plan.

Current estate-tax benchmark & statutory clocks

Federal exclusion: $15,480,000 for 2027; California has no separate estate tax under current law. The 2040s amount and any law change are unknown. Verify trustee notice (60 days), change-in-ownership statement (150 days), Form 706 (generally 9 months), homeowners’ exemption and Prop 19 deadlines at transfer.
