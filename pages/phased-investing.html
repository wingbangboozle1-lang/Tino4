Treasury staging tool

Model 24–36 months of 3-month to 2-year Treasuries beside equity entry and renewal risk.

Headline and risk

Phasing sale proceeds into equities can reduce immediate exposure to an early fall, but can lag a rising market. Treasury interest resets at reinvestment; lower renewal yields, taxes, inflation, and delayed equity exposure can change the result.

What the fixed scenarios show

Across the published set, a 24-month window is ahead of a lump sum in 5 of 9 scenarios with a median advantage of about 3% and a worst case of about -5%. A 36-month window is ahead in 7 of 9 with a median of about 6% and a worst case of about -7%. The pattern is not 'staging wins'. It is that staging buys protection against a crash that arrives EARLY, and pays for it with the spread between equity and Treasury returns when the market simply rises. A crash that arrives as the window closes is close to a wash, and a crash after the window closes hits both paths almost equally. Reserved per the decision-log rule: these are assumption-driven outcomes at a single 60-month date, not probabilities, and they say nothing about any household's amounts.

The uncomfortable result worth keeping

In the 1929-shaped cases, staging helps but does not rescue the household. Even with a 36-month window, a 1929-shaped path leaves the staged portfolio roughly a third behind where a calm market would have put it. If the household cannot tolerate that, the answer is not a longer staging window; it is a smaller allocation to equities, a larger reserve, or a different decision about the property. No staging schedule converts a 25-year recovery into a safe one.

Limits

This simplified path applies one instantaneous drawdown at the selected month, with a constant annual equity-price drift and Treasury yield otherwise. It does not model a historical sequence or recovery, dividends, fees, taxes, withdrawals, asset correlations, property costs, or changing Treasury yields. A 57% or 89% shock compresses a multi-year index decline into one event and is a stress input only, not a forecast or probability.
