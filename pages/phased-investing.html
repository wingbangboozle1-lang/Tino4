Treasury staging and phased stock entry

A source-calibrated, assumption-led stress comparison for 24–36 month Treasury parking followed by gradual equity purchases. It is not an individualized investment recommendation.

Reinvestment risk belongs beside the headline

Treasury bills and notes have fixed auction rates for their terms. When a short holding matures, the next auction yield may be lower; staging can therefore reduce early equity exposure yet leave more money earning less. A rising market can also make phased buying lag a lump-sum investment.

Staging band used in the comparison

The tool models a 3-month Treasury bill through a 2-year Treasury note band (3M–2Y) held for 24–36 months, followed by scheduled equity purchases. TreasuryDirect states that bill rates are fixed at auction for terms of 4–52 weeks and that a 2-year note is available among note maturities; interest is federally taxable and exempt from state and local taxes. Which specific securities and maturities suit the estate is an adviser decision, not an output of this tool.

Crash-entry what-if — normalized units, not dollars

The starting portfolio is normalized to 100 index units so no household amount is embedded. Historical drawdown endpoints are sourced, but the path between peak and trough is synthetic and linear. Controls model a rising pre-crash market, separate first-year and renewal Treasury yields, and inflation-adjusted purchasing power. Enter the Treasury yield net of estimated taxes and fees; the tool does not calculate taxes. This is not a historical total-return replay and sends or stores no calculator inputs.

0% rates are neutral isolation settings, not Treasury quotes or forecasts.

Model boundary: equal monthly equity purchases from the initial 100 units; Treasury reserve accrues the entered yield by month and uses the renewal yield after month 12; the final purchase includes any accrued reserve. Equity grows at the entered drift until the crash, declines linearly to the selected drawdown, then stays flat at its trough. Real units divide by the selected constant annual inflation factor. There are no dividends, fees, taxes, post-trough recovery, withdrawals, property costs or probabilities. The zero-rate and zero-inflation defaults isolate timing only; they are not forecasts.

Ladder reinvestment risk — what maturing rungs cost you

A 24–36 month parking plan is a ladder: several short Treasury rungs mature at different times and each one is reinvested at whatever auction rate exists then. This panel isolates that effect. It runs the same crash and the same staging schedule twice — once with every renewal at the first-year yield, once with the later rungs repriced lower — and reports the reserve interest earned before any equity outcome is applied. Starting capital is normalized to 100 units; yields are entered net of estimated federal tax, state tax is not applicable to Treasury interest, and fees are not modeled.

Default yields are illustrative round settings, not a Treasury quote. Read the current auction results before relying on any yield.

Interpretation

A staged plan can buy more shares during an early falling market because later installments purchase at lower index values. If the same decline starts after staging ends, all proceeds are already exposed. A positive pre-crash drift control tests one rising-market opportunity-cost scenario, but it is a constant synthetic rate rather than a historical return series. Use actual after-tax yields and an adviser-approved investment policy before making a real allocation.
