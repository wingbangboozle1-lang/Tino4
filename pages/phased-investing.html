Treasury parking & phased stocks

Current law, owner figures, stated assumptions and open questions, kept apart on purpose.

What would change the answer?
  • The trust instrument or estate plan does not permit occupancy or requires a sale.
  • Proposition 19 eligibility, the transfer-date tax base, or the whole-estate tax picture differs from the planning assumptions.
  • Written rent, insurance, repair, debt and utility evidence makes net rental cash flow materially better than the current illustrative case.
  • The inheritor cannot or does not want to live in the inherited home; that makes sell-and-reinvest the fallback to test.

A mixed path: after a hypothetical sale, temporarily ladder proceeds across short U.S. Treasury maturities, then move them into a diversified stock allocation over 24–36 months. Compare that with investing the lump sum immediately.

The trade-off beside the headline

Phasing can reduce the amount exposed to a sudden equity fall during the entry window, but it does not remove risk. It can lag a rising market, delays equity exposure, and exposes cash to reinvestment risk as each short Treasury matures. Treasury interest is federally taxable and generally exempt from state and local income tax. This tool is pretax for equities and uses a user-entered net Treasury yield; it is not a tax forecast.

The model is a transparent illustration, not a historical replay. The two preset lenses are deliberately synthetic and inspired by, but do not reconstruct, the 1929–1930s or 2008–09 market episodes. Their drawdown/recovery values are editable assumptions, not historical returns. No verified full-period total-return stock series is used here. Treasury yields at a future inheritance date are unknown; today's short rates cannot be locked for decades. Principal protection assumes eligible Treasury securities are held to maturity; selling before maturity can produce a gain or loss.

Decision use

Ask the adviser to run the same comparison with the actual net proceeds, an approved asset allocation, a Treasury ladder and tax estimates. Compare several crash start dates, including a crash after the staging period. Do not treat “phased” as a guaranteed safer or higher-return strategy.
