STRESS TESTS

Treasury staging tool

Model 24–36 months of 3-month to 2-year Treasuries beside equity entry and renewal risk.

Headline and risk

Phasing sale proceeds into equities can reduce immediate exposure to an early fall, but can lag a rising market. Treasury interest resets at reinvestment; lower renewal yields, taxes, inflation, and delayed equity exposure can change the result.

Limits

This simplified path applies one instantaneous drawdown at the selected month, with a constant annual equity-price drift and Treasury yield otherwise. It does not model a historical sequence or recovery, dividends, fees, taxes, withdrawals, asset correlations, property costs, or changing Treasury yields. A 57% or 89% shock compresses a multi-year index decline into one event and is a stress input only, not a forecast or probability.

What the staging path is, in numbers

Park the net proceeds of a sale in a ladder of three-month to two-year Treasury maturities for 24 to 36 months after the transfer, then move into equities in equal tranches - twelve quarterly steps in the model. The point is not to beat the market; it is to stop a single decision date from deciding the next thirty years, and to pay for that insurance in a measurable way.

Reinvestment risk, next to the headline

Staging is not free. In a market that keeps rising the un-deployed money buys in higher: the model's own arithmetic gives $1,386,490 of price increase on the waiting money over the 30-month window at a 10 percent drift, and the waiting leg earns $64,660 in bills over the same span. In a crisis the safe leg earns close to nothing, because the rates on bills are cut in the same weeks the shares fall: the federal funds target went from 5.25 percent in September 2007 to 0 to 0.25 percent by December 2008, which is the reason a 2008-shaped crash is the best case for waiting and the worst case for the yield earned while waiting.

The decision rule this supports

Write the rule before the money exists: stage for a fixed window, buy on a fixed schedule, and do not extend the window because the market looks about to fall. A staging plan that is allowed to grow is a market-timing plan wearing a policy document, and the two rows above show what the discipline costs when nothing happens.

Mechanics worth fixing in advance

Bills are bought at auction through TreasuryDirect in denominations the site states, maturities run four, eight, thirteen, seventeen and twenty-six weeks for bills and two to ten years for notes, and interest on both is taxable federally and exempt from California income tax. A two-year note held to maturity returns par; sold early it can return less. None of that is a household fact and none of it needs to be guessed - it is read from the Treasury pages at the day of purchase.
