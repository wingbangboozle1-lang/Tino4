STRESS TESTS

Treasury staging tool

Test a 24–36 month short-Treasury path against immediate equity entry without publishing household figures.

Headline and risk

A 24–36 month schedule can reduce immediate equity exposure if a severe decline occurs while proceeds remain in short Treasuries. It can lag a rising market, and each maturity creates reinvestment risk. No household-specific staging result is published.

One-page policy rule

Before proceeds arrive, write the target asset allocation, cash reserve, purchase dates, maturity plan, acceptable drawdown, and the conditions that would change the schedule. Treat a decision to wait as an investment position with a measurable opportunity cost.

Read both sides of the result

An early crash may make waiting look protective because less has reached equities. A crash after the schedule is complete affects both paths more similarly; a rising market can make staging lag. Compare drawdown and terminal wealth, and show the Treasury yield reset beside the equity path. The model does not include taxes, dividends, fees, withdrawals or any property cash flow.
