STRESS TESTS

Black-swan conditions

Verified crash history, a 1928-2025 annual-return stress table for every entry strategy including the 24-36 month Treasury parking plan, and a local-only interactive stress tool.

Why a separate black-swan section

A 2040-2050 transfer followed by a 30-plus-year horizon crosses many regimes: growth and inflation are never consistent for 50 years. This section uses verified historical observations (Federal Reserve History) and a transcribed 1928-2025 annual-return series (NYU Stern compilation, flagged secondary) to stress-test how each path behaves from a crash-time entry, instead of assuming an average year.

What the stress table shows

The table computes each strategy from the actual annual record. Ratios below are staged-versus-lump ending wealth. Parking 24 months in Treasuries and then phasing comes out 2.22x from a 1929 entry, 1.57x from a 2000 entry, 1.19x from a 2008 entry, and 0.74x in the fast 2020 recovery. Dollar-cost averaging without parking sits between the two. No one can know in advance which regime the transfer lands in; the phased path is insurance whose premium is the upside missed in V-shaped recoveries.

Reinvestment risk, quantified

The Treasury-parking plan has two tails: yields can fall while parked (reinvestment risk — the parked money returns less than the yield it was bought at), and equities can run away before the phase-in completes (timing cost). The 1928-2025 record contains both. The phased-investing page explains the mechanics and the Treasury state-tax exemption that matters for a California heir.

Model limits

Annual granularity ignores intra-year sequencing; the T-bill column proxies the 3M-2Y Treasury ladder; dividends are included in the S&P total-return series; no taxes or transaction costs are modeled. The series is a secondary academic compilation — verify headline years against primary market data before acting.
