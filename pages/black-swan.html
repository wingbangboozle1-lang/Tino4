STRESS TESTS

Black-swan conditions

Stress sequences of growth, inflation, crashes, recovery and reinvestment rather than extending one constant rate across decades.

Black-swan framing

A 50-year horizon will not follow one stable growth or inflation rate. Historical crises show that inflation, equity markets, housing, interest rates and liquidity can move in different regimes. Historical anchors are stress references, not probabilities or forecasts.

Stress design

Use multi-year blocks for expansion, recession, high inflation, deflation, recovery and renewed shocks. Move crash timing to before, during and after the staging period. Include a joint housing-and-equity decline, a prolonged recovery, falling short-rate renewals, higher insurance and maintenance costs, rental vacancy, and constrained liquidity. Label every unobserved assumption; the historical sources do not provide a 50-year forecast.

Interpretation and limits

The selected regime path is a transparent sensitivity, not a statistical forecast. It does not incorporate household tax returns, property cash flows, insurance quotes, legal eligibility, transaction costs, dividends or changing personal circumstances. Compare percentiles and drawdowns only after the assumptions have been documented privately; do not publish a household-specific result.

Research framework only; not legal, tax, insurance, appraisal, investment, or property-management advice.

Recheck official authorities, source availability and law at the actual decision date.
