STRESS TESTS

Black-swan conditions

Stress regimes should mimic historical mechanisms, not claim to predict their timing or probability.

Stress scenarios to run
RegimeEvidenceHousehold stress designEvidenceOfficial source / scope
1929 crash anchorFederal Reserve History reports the Dow fell 89% peak-to-trough and did not regain its prior high until 1954; that is a historical price-index path, not a diversified portfolio return.Use an 89% stress anchor only as an explicitly labeled scenario; test crash during and after staging, not as a probability.HistoricalFederal Reserve History — Stock Market Crash of 1929
2007–09 Great Recession anchorFederal Reserve History reports an approximately 57% S&P 500 peak-to-trough decline and approximately 30% average U.S. home-price decline from prior peaks.Compare an early-staging and post-staging stress; do not treat national indexes as a property or portfolio forecast.HistoricalFederal Reserve History — The Great Recession · SEC Investor.gov — Stocks
Deflation and income stressThe Great Depression history describes deflation and severe economic contraction.Stress falling nominal rents or income, vacancies, debt service, and fixed costs together.HistoricalFederal Reserve History — The Great Depression · U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — CPI
Inflation / stagflationFederal Reserve history documents a high-inflation period and policy response; historical inflation can be uneven.Stress simultaneous high inflation, high financing/insurance cost, weak real returns and delayed maintenance.HistoricalFederal Reserve History — The Great Inflation · U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — CPI
Housing and equities fall togetherNational indices cannot describe a specific property or household balance sheet.Test lower home value and lower equities at once; include sale liquidity, debt and rent interruption.GuidanceFederal Housing Finance Agency — House Price Index · Federal Reserve History — The Great Recession · SEC Investor.gov — Stocks
Long-horizon baselineCBO and historical series are bounded; no official institution forecasts an individual home or portfolio over a 50-year horizon.Use irregular sequences of regimes rather than a single annual growth rate; label all future paths as assumptions.GuidanceCongressional Budget Office — Long-Term Budget Outlook · Congressional Budget Office — Economic Outlook · U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — CPI
