Economic baselines

The documented record on variability - prices, house values, equity drawdowns and policy rates - and the block path the model uses instead of one growth rate for fifty years.

Why this page exists

A fifty-year plan is not one number repeated fifty times. Prices have moved by more than ten percent in a year and by less than minus ten percent in another, home values have fallen by a fifth over fourteen quarters, and equity indexes have taken twenty-five years to return to a previous high. The rows below are the documented record; the block path used by the model is assembled from shapes like these, and it is a planning device, not a forecast.

How the model turns this into a path, and how it does not

The block path gives each of the next thirty years a home-growth rate, a price rate, a rent rate, an equity rate and a bill yield, taken in uneven blocks rather than one average, then loops. Every table that uses it says so in its evidence column. What the model never does is assign a probability to a bust, forecast a level, or present a single number as the expected outcome; the official sources above describe what happened, not what will.
