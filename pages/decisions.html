Decision analysis

Live in it, rent it, or sell it — with the tax consequences attached

Four strategies, one model, identical assumptions, run from the inheritance year to 2065. Everything below is generated by tools/model.py: there are no hand-typed results.

Result

Sell the Cupertino house shortly after death and reinvest the proceeds; keep the San Francisco building. Base case: $29,770,260 of 2065 net worth against $25,388,590 for living in it and $25,325,660 for renting it out. The margin over "live in it" is $4,381,670 on a hold basis and $7,294,720 after liquidation costs and capital gains tax.

Renting the house out and living in it are effectively tied — a difference of $62,930 in 2065. That is a useful finding: you are not giving up much by choosing the living arrangement you actually prefer.

Read the break-even honestly

Bay Area house prices have historically compounded faster than 5.5% nominal over long windows, and a 4% after-tax portfolio return is pessimistic. If you believe both of those at once — Cupertino above 5.46% and a portfolio at 4% — then keeping the house wins. With the model's own 5.5% portfolio return, the house would need 7.44% a year to catch up. The decision is a bet on appreciation versus diversified returns, not a bet on the tax code.

Action list

  1. Now — confirm residency facts

    Which property is the parents' principal residence, and is a homeowners' exemption filed there? This single fact determines whether Prop 19 relief exists at all.

  2. Now — get two real insurance quotes per property

    The $1,500 estimate is the weakest input in the cash-flow model.

  3. Now — confirm the trust actually holds the real property

    A trust avoids probate only for assets titled to it. A deed into the trust now is far cheaper than a probate later.

  4. Now — record the basis

    Keep the original purchase documents. The step-up at death is only as good as the paperwork that proves what the parents paid and what was improved.

  5. Do not sell either property before death

    A sale by the parents realises their capital gain and destroys the step-up. The cheapest exit is the one that happens after death.

  6. At death — get a dated appraisal before anything else

    It sets the new basis and the Prop 19 market value.

  7. Within 12 months — decide occupancy

    Move in and file the exemption, or accept reassessment. There is no extension.

  8. At each 5-year review — re-run tools/model.py

    Update appreciation, rents and rates; the ranking is sensitive to the first two.
