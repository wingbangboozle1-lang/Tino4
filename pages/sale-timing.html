SELL PATH

Sale timing and the tax year of the inheritance

Why a sale in the same tax year as the transfer is modeled first, what the sale actually costs line by line, what a written valuation has to settle, and the exact Proposition 19 occupancy rule with its break-even.

Do this first: price a sale in the tax year of the inheritance

A sale in the same tax year as the transfer is modeled first because it is the only branch whose income-tax answer is close to settled before the numbers arrive. Inherited property generally takes a date-of-death value as its income-tax basis, and the IRS states that a sale soon after the date of death therefore usually shows little or no gain. Every later year of holding converts tax-free appreciation into a taxable gain, and renting the house converts part of that gain into depreciation recapture. Price the same-year sale first, then ask what a later sale costs — not the other way round.

The number the timing question turns on

Under these assumptions, waiting five years and renting costs about $-389,490 of net proceeds relative to selling in the transfer year — before counting five years of property tax, insurance, maintenance and vacancy on one side against five years of rent on the other. Timing is decided before investing, because it sets how much of the proceeds are actually investable.

Obtain a written valuation of the inherited house at the date of death

A written appraisal dated to the valuation date, with comparable sales and a reasonableness range, is the single document that makes the sale-timing, estate-tax and basis analyses comparable. A listing price, an insurance figure or an online estimate is not that document.

The Proposition 19 occupancy rule, and the allowance carried forward

Read the exclusion and the base-year-value transfer as two different benefits with two different tests. Neither is available on paper alone, and neither survives a decision to rent immediately.

The Proposition 19 occupancy rule, and the allowance carried forward
ElementCurrent official ruleWhy it matters to this decisionEvidence statusOfficial source / scope
The occupancy rule, stated exactlyThe Proposition 19 family-home exclusion applies to a principal residence of the transferor, the transfer must be to a child or grandchild, the excluded amount is the factored base year value plus the adjusted value-limit amount, and the transferee must occupy the home as a principal residence within one year of the transfer — the BOE states plainly that there are no exceptions.So the exclusion is not a rental-planning tool: it is available only if the household intends to live in the house, long term.VerifiedCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19 · California BOE — Letter to Assessors 2026/026 (Proposition 19)
Death and trust transfers countThe BOE letter to assessors confirms that a transfer includes a transfer at death and a transfer through a trust, so the inherited-house path is inside the rule rather than beside it.The one-year occupancy clock runs from the date of death or the transfer, not from the day the paperwork is finished.VerifiedCalifornia BOE — Letter to Assessors 2026/026 (Proposition 19)
The value limit caps the benefitThe excluded amount is the factored base year value immediately before the transfer plus the adjusted limit (the 2025-2027 amount is $1,044,586), and any excess of full cash value over that sum is added to the base and then indexed annually.On the modeled Property A figures the value limit is what does the work: the relief is worth about $21,670 a year, not the whole gap between the old and the new tax base.ProjectionCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2 · Owner-supplied records — Property A (privacy-rounded to nearest $10)
Occupancy must be continuousIf the transferee stops using the home as a principal residence, the exclusion is removed at the next lien date and the enrolled value reverts to the date-of-transfer full cash value factored forward — the relief is pulled back retroactively, not merely stopped.A plan that occupies for three years and then rents is not a plan with a preserved base; it is a plan with a delayed reassessment.VerifiedCalifornia BOE — Letter to Assessors 2026/026 (Proposition 19) · California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2
The claims, both of themThe exclusion claim is generally filed within three years of the transfer or before a transfer to a third party, and the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption must be claimed within one year; a late claim generally gives relief only from the year of filing forward.Calendar both dates independently, and budget the higher bill until a claim is processed.VerifiedCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §218 — Homeowners' property tax exemption
The separate base-year-value transferA claimant who sells a principal residence may transfer its factored base year value to a replacement principal residence purchased or newly built within two years, anywhere in California, with the value above the original added to the transferred base; up to three transfers are allowed, subject to the age-55, disability or disaster conditions.This is the allowance carried forward — a second, separate benefit with its own conditions, available to the heir as owner, not to the estate.VerifiedCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19
Break-even for occupyingOccupying preserves about $21,670 a year of property tax under the modeled 2045 figures, roughly $237,320 over ten years. Letting the house instead earns gross rent net of an operating load that already excludes the property-tax line, so the two paths cross at about $1,900 of gross monthly rent.Above that rent the tax-and-carry arithmetic favors letting; below it, occupying wins. The comparison ignores the outside rent an occupant would otherwise pay, which is usually the larger term and points the other way.ProjectionOwner-supplied records — Property A (privacy-rounded to nearest $10) · California BOE — Proposition 19 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2 · IRS Publication 527 — Residential Rental Property
Multi-unit buildings are tested per unitThe BOE guidance states that each unit of a multi-unit property is separately eligible or ineligible, and that a property is tested as a family home only where the transferor owned and occupied it.Property B's rented units are not covered by the transferor's occupancy; do not carry a single-family conclusion across to a three-unit building.VerifiedCalifornia BOE — Letter to Assessors 2026/026 (Proposition 19) · California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2

How to use this page

Run the same-year sale case first. Then decide in writing whether the household wants the property-tax benefit of occupancy, the income-tax benefit of an early sale, or a mixed path — and only then write the investing policy for whatever money is actually left. Re-run this page with the real valuation date, the assessor's determination and the CPA's basis schedule before anything is signed.
