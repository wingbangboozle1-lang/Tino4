Transfer tax, recording charges and the change-of-ownership filing
Charge or ruleWhat the published schedule establishesConsequence for a sale or transferEvidenceOfficial source / scope
Documentary transfer tax is charged per 500 dollars of considerationThe city-county schedule states an amount per 500 dollars of consideration in tiers that rise with price, with a reduced per-500 amount for rent-restricted affordable housing.A sale cannot be priced as if one flat rate applied; the local rate, the tier and the rounding convention must be read from the schedule for the jurisdiction and the year.Verified ruleSan Francisco County Recorder — fees to record a document · California statute — documentary transfer tax
The applicable tier can reach the whole considerationUnder the published schedule the tier amount per 500 dollars applies to the entire consideration once the price crosses a tier boundary, rather than only to the excess above it.A price just above a boundary can cost far more in tax than one just below it — more than the difference in price. Have escrow confirm the method for the parcel and year, and never re-time or split a sale price to sit just over a line.Verified ruleSan Francisco County Recorder — fees to record a document
Recording charges and the real-estate fraud feeA base charge covers the first page, with per-page and nonstandard-page charges on top, plus a fee on documents affecting title that is not charged when recorded concurrently with a transfer that pays transfer tax.Closing cost is document-driven: page count, the number of instruments and concurrent filing all change the total, so it belongs in the escrow estimate rather than a rule of thumb.Verified ruleSan Francisco County Recorder — fees to record a document · California statute — recording fees
The local affordable-housing parcel fee and its exemptionsThe per-parcel fee applies to declarations of change in ownership and is capped per transaction, with an exemption for documents that pay transfer tax and for a residential dwelling transferred to an owner-occupier — which must be declared on the face of the instrument or on a coversheet.Exemptions are claimed in writing at recording; an undeclared exemption is paid. Confirm who signs the declaration, and do not assume a transfer at death is exempt.Verified ruleSan Francisco County Recorder — fees to record a document
The change-of-ownership screening formA fee is charged when a transfer document is recorded without the required change-of-ownership statement; the state statute separately sets a filing deadline for specified transfers by reason of death.Missing the form is a fee that buys nothing, and the death-transfer filing window runs whether or not a sale is planned.Verified ruleSan Francisco County Recorder — fees to record a document · California Revenue and Taxation Code §480
Whether a transfer at death pays transfer taxThat question is answered by the local ordinance and the recorder, not by a general rule; no jurisdiction-neutral answer is published on this site.Obtain the recorder's written position before relying on an exempt transfer, and keep separate the transfer-tax question and the change-in-ownership question.Not verifiedCalifornia statute — documentary transfer tax · California Revenue and Taxation Code §480
The second building's jurisdictionNo official schedule for the anticipated-trust property's county could be confirmed for this build, so no amount is published for it and no neighbouring-city add-on is assumed.Read the county ordinance and the recorder's schedule, then price them into the private model beside the city figures.MissingCalifornia BOE — County assessor contacts
The top-tier increase and the measure's real nameThe city's own page records that the November 2020 measure raising the top two transfer-tax tiers was Proposition I. A measure referred to as 'Prop H' could not be matched to any property, transfer-tax or landlord measure in the official material reviewed.Cite the measure that exists. Before modeling a proposal under the other name, confirm jurisdiction, year and ballot number and read the official text; nothing on this site depends on an unidentified measure.Not verifiedSan Francisco — Housing Stability Fund Oversight Board (about) · California Secretary of State — Qualified ballot measures
Carrying the Proposition 19 allowance forward
Fact to holdCurrent official positionWhy it decides the outcomeEvidenceOfficial source / scope
What the exclusion actually preservesA qualifying family-home transfer keeps the transferor's factored base year value plus an adjusted value-limit amount; the excess of market value over that sum is added to the new base, and the taxable value cannot exceed market value.The allowance, not the low base, is the decisive number: it is the only term that shrinks the reassessment, and it is capped at the property's value.Verified ruleCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2 · California BOE — Proposition 19
The allowance is indexed on a published scheduleThe letter to assessors gives the amount in effect through mid-February 2027 and states that it is adjusted every two years using the federal housing index for California, with the next adjustment due in 2027.For a transfer decades away no one can publish the amount in advance: project it as a labelled assumption, and read the official figure for the actual year of transfer before a claim is prepared.Verified ruleCalifornia BOE — Letter to Assessors 2025/009 · Federal Housing Finance Agency — House Price Index
Prior claims reduce what is availableThe statute limits what may be excluded across family-home transfers of the same property, including later transfers, and treats subsequent transfers under its own conditions.A property whose transferor already used an exclusion may carry less relief than the published allowance implies; the assessor's claim history decides it, not the statutory maximum.MissingCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2
Occupancy and filing conditionsThe claimant must occupy the home as a principal residence and file the homeowners' exemption within the required period, and the property-tax claim has a separate deadline; relief is disallowed if the conditions fail.A claim made for a house that is then rented is the fact pattern the conditions are written against: the cost is reassessment plus the loss of the filing position.Verified ruleCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §218 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2
Renting after the claim is a separate questionThe rules distinguish a qualifying owner-occupied transfer from later rental use, and conversion consequences are property-specific.Do not treat a live-in plan as revocable at will: model the later-rental branch before claiming, and retain the written determination with the trust records.GuidanceCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2
