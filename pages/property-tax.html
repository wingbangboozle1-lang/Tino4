PROPERTY

Property tax & history

A clear distinction between current law, missing evidence and illustrative scenarios.

Three values, three different purposes

Market value measures a sale valuation. Assessed value determines a property-tax base. Income-tax basis measures taxable sale gain. They are not interchangeable. The bill history is supplied by the household and has not been officially reconciled.

THE NUMBERS

Property tax & history

Current law, owner figures, stated assumptions and open questions, kept apart on purpose.

Three different numbers are called "value" and they are not interchangeable

Market value is what a buyer would pay. Assessed value is the Prop 13 base that the bill is calculated on. Tax basis is what the income tax is calculated on when you sell. A federal step-up resets the third and does nothing to the second. The Proposition 19 exclusion resets the second and does nothing to the third. Confusing them is the single most expensive mistake available in this decision.

The rules that govern the bill

Status says what kind of evidence a row rests on: Verified means an official source states it, Owner means you supplied it and it is not independently confirmed, Projection means arithmetic on rounded inputs under a stated assumption, Assumption means an invented planning parameter, and Missing means the cell is blank because no evidence exists. A linked source supports the rule, not a private fact.

The rules that govern the bill
RuleWhat the official source saysWhat it means hereStatusOfficial source / scope
The 1% levyOne percent of assessed value plus the rate needed for voter-approved bonded debt.A's effective rate is 1.34% of assessed value and B's is 2.14%. Neither is 1%.VerifiedBOE publication 29
The 2% capAnnual increases in the base year value are limited to no more than 2%.Applied every year in every projection on this site, including in the crash years, because that is what the cap does.VerifiedBOE publication 29
The cap is not a ceiling on the billThe limit applies to the base year value, not to charges and direct assessments.A new bond or a new direct assessment can raise the bill by more than 2% in a single year.VerifiedBOE publication 29
Reassessment on transferProperty is reassessed to market value on a change in ownership.Renting A triggers this at the transfer date. Occupying it does not, if the family-home exclusion applies.VerifiedBOE publication 29 BOE Proposition 19
The family-home exclusionThe base may carry over, up to the factored base plus the biennially adjusted allowance, if an eligible child occupies the home as a principal residence.This is the mechanism that creates almost all of the occupancy advantage.VerifiedBOE Prop 19 fact sheet BOE LTA 2026/026
Continual useIf the home stops being the transferee's principal residence, the exclusion is removed.Move out later and the reassessment arrives then, computed from the transfer date.VerifiedBOE LTA 2026/026
Filing windowsClaim the exclusion within three years, and file the homeowners exemption within one year to get the exclusion from the transfer date.Miss the one-year window and the benefit starts when you file, not when you inherited.VerifiedBOE Prop 19 fact sheet

What the history does not tell you

No county roll, bill or exemption record has been read. A supplied total that repeats in consecutive years, a year with no bill, and a missing current-year bill for B are all recorded as gaps rather than explained. They may be perfectly ordinary: fiscal-year labelling, a supplemental assessment, a refund or a payment timing difference would all produce the same appearance. The correct next step is to pull the original bills and the assessor's value notice for each year, which is question Q3 and Q4 on the questions page.
