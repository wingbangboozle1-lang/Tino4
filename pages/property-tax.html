Basis, assessed value and estate value are three different numbers

Keep three ledgers: the income-tax basis (generally date-of-death fair market value, subject to exceptions), the property-tax assessed value (base year value with the statutory inflation factor, reassessment and exclusions), and the estate value used for any estate-level filing. A change in one does not change the others, and treating them as the same figure is a common source of wrong answers.

Worked example of the exclusion arithmetic, on rounded figures

A home whose capped taxable value starts at roughly one twentieth of its market value and whose value at transfer is modelled at $5,435,870 cannot carry its whole base forward: the cap is the prior taxable value plus the indexed allowance, and everything above it is added back. The saving is real, and it is smaller than most summaries imply.
