Property-tax rules

California property-tax mechanics that matter for a future trust transfer, with no address, assessor record or property-specific tax history disclosed.

Critical distinction

Income-tax basis and property-tax assessed value are different systems. A date-of-death basis analysis does not by itself determine the assessed value or a Proposition 19 exclusion.

California property-tax rules and missing parcel facts

The California BOE warns that statutes and rules change; verify the law and assessor's calculation at the actual transfer.

Mechanics that drive a year-by-year tax projection

Taxable value each yearRTC §51 sets taxable value as the lesser of (a) the base-year value inflated by the October-to-October California CPI change, capped at 2% per year, or (b) current full cash value.The enrolled value can fall below the inflated base if market value declines, which is why a flat or falling housing case is not automatically the same as a rising tax case.VerifiedCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §51 — Taxable value of real property
Base-year reset on transferA change in ownership generally re-establishes the base-year value at current market value unless an exclusion applies.The transfer is the event that resets the base; the exclusion is the only thing that preserves the lower base.VerifiedCalifornia BOE Publication 29 — California Property Tax · California BOE — Proposition 19
Homeowners' exemptionThe exemption is $7,000 of the dwelling's full value and does not apply to rented, vacant, secondary or vacation property on the lien date.On a 1% general levy that is a modest reduction per year, but losing it can also signal a failed principal-residence condition elsewhere in the analysis.VerifiedCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §218 — Homeowners' property tax exemption · California BOE Publication 29 — California Property Tax
Filing dates that recur every yearJanuary 1 is the lien date; February 15 is the legal deadline for most timely exemption claims; April 10 and December 10 are the last days to pay the second and first secured instalments without penalty.A late claim can reduce a benefit even where eligibility exists, so the calendar matters as much as the rule.VerifiedCalifornia BOE Publication 29 — California Property Tax
Rental use and the residence conditionThe family-home exclusion requires the property to become the transferee's principal residence within one year and the transferee to file the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption within that year.A plan that rents the property out immediately may fail the residence condition, so 'rent it for a year then move in' is not automatically equivalent to moving in.VerifiedCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §218 — Homeowners' property tax exemption
Value-limit testThe exclusion applies to the transferor's principal residence subject to a value limit expressed as the current taxable value plus an adjusted amount, with the excess above the limit added to the transferred base.Eligibility is not all-or-nothing; a property above the limit can still receive partial relief, which changes the modelled tax materially.VerifiedCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2

Private records to request

For each asset separately, obtain the assessor roll, full secured tax bill, supplemental assessment history, deed and trust transfer documents, homeowners' exemption status, parcel-specific direct levies, and any filed Proposition 19 claims. Do not publish a parcel number or tax bill in this public repository.

Rate caveat

The model derives Property A's rate of about 1.353% and Property B's rate of about 2.136% from the owner's own bills. Neither is an official county rate. Property B's implied rate is far above the 1% general levy plus voter-approved bonded indebtedness, which is flagged for review. Obtain the actual tax-rate-area rate from the county before relying on any projection.
