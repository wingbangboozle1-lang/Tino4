Questions & missing evidence

Questions, the answers official sources already give, and how to close the gaps.

How to use this register

This page separates three things that are often mixed together: the questions only private facts can answer, the answers official sources already give, and the method for discovering what is missing. Nothing on this page is a household figure; every row links to the official source for the narrow point it makes.

Questions official sources already answer

What is the current federal estate-tax filing threshold?Read 2026-11-17: IRS lists a filing threshold of $15,000,000 for 2026 and $13,990,000 for 2025, and IRS inflation guidance for 2027 sets the basic exclusion at $15,480,000. The test is estate level and adds adjusted taxable gifts.Use the year-of-death table and the IRS annual adjustment for that year; it is not a per-heir allowance and not a 2050 forecast.Verified ruleIRS — Estate tax · IRS — 2027 tax inflation adjustments
What is the top federal estate-tax rate?Read 2026-11-17: the Instructions for Form 706 (Rev. 7-2026) unified rate schedule applies 40% to taxable amounts over $1,000,000, and lists a 2026 credit equivalent of $5,945,800.Apply the schedule for the actual year of death with the CPA; do not mix a rate from one year with an exclusion from another.Verified ruleIRS — Instructions for Form 706 · IRS — Estate-tax filing FAQs
When is the federal estate return due, and can it be extended?Read 2026-11-17: IRS states Form 706 is due nine months after date of death and that an automatic six-month extension to file is available by filing Form 4768 on or before the due date; an extension to file is not an extension to pay.Calendar both dates at death and plan funding before the window opens.Verified ruleIRS — Estate-tax filing FAQs · IRS — About Form 706
How is portability elected?Read 2026-11-17: IRS states the estate's representative must file Form 706 and it must be timely, even when the estate is below the filing threshold; Revenue Procedure 2022-32 provides a simplified method within five years where no filing requirement exists.Ask now about prior taxable gifts and any deceased-spouse unused exclusion; the election is lost if the return is late.Verified ruleIRS — Estate-tax filing FAQs · IRS — Estate tax
Does California impose an estate or inheritance tax?Read 2026-11-17: the State Controller states no California estate tax return is required for deaths on or after January 1, 2005, the state death tax credit was eliminated on that date, and the California generation-skipping transfer tax does not apply after December 31, 2004.Separate state estate tax from income tax on a sale; recheck current law at the actual date.Verified ruleCalifornia State Controller — Estate tax · California statute — death-transfer tax
How are California capital gains taxed, and when does the 1% add-on apply?Read 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16: FTB states California has no lower capital-gain rate; the 2025 Form 540 line 62 instructions compute a 1% Behavioral Health Services Tax on taxable income above $1,000,000 in whole dollars, and §17043 sets that threshold in statute.Recheck the instructions for the actual sale year; each $100,000 of taxable income above the threshold adds $1,000 of this tax before other return effects.Verified ruleFTB — Capital gains and losses · FTB — 2025 Form 540 instructions · California statute — additional tax on high taxable income
What is the inherited income-tax basis?Read 2026-11-17: IRS states the basis of property inherited from a decedent is generally fair market value on the date of death, or the alternate valuation date only if Form 706 is filed and the election is made; a consistency rule can require reporting a basis consistent with the finally determined estate-tax value.Obtain a written date-of-death appraisal and keep the estate's basis reporting consistent.Verified ruleIRS — Gifts and inheritances FAQ · IRS Publication 551 — Basis of Assets
How does California value property for tax purposes each year?Read 2026-11-17: Revenue and Taxation Code §51 sets taxable value at the lesser of the base year value compounded by an inflation factor that may not exceed 2 percent in any assessment year, or full cash value as of the lien date after declines in value.Apply the statute to the parcel's own base year and roll; a 2 percent cap is a ceiling, not an entitlement.Verified ruleCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §51
What is the current Proposition 19 value-limit amount?Read 2026-11-17: the state board's Letter to Assessors 2025/009 sets the cap at factored base year value plus $1,044,586 for transfers from February 16, 2025 through February 15, 2027, after $1,000,000 (2021-2023) and $1,022,600 (2023-2025); the next adjustment is February 2027.Use the amount in force on the transfer date; do not project the index forward.Verified ruleCalifornia BOE — Letter to Assessors 2025/009 · California BOE — Proposition 19
What occupancy and filing conditions does Proposition 19 impose?Read 2026-11-17: under §63.2 the home must be the transferor's principal residence and become the transferee's principal residence within one year, the transferee must file for the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption within one year, and if the exclusion later ends the base year value established at the change in ownership, adjusted annually under §51(a)(1), is enrolled.Claim only if long-term occupancy is genuinely intended; a later move can unwind the benefit.Verified ruleCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2 · California BOE — Proposition 19
What is the maximum documentary transfer-tax rate the statute authorises?Read 2026-11-17: Revenue and Taxation Code §11911 authorises a county rate of 55 cents for each $500 or fractional part of consideration above $100 (the same rate as $110 per $100,000), and a city inside such a county may impose half the county rate with a credit against the county tax.Read the enacted schedule for the property's own jurisdiction at sale; authorisation is not imposition.Verified ruleCalifornia statute — documentary transfer tax · California statute — recording fees
Can a primary residence go into a like-kind exchange?Read 2026-11-16: IRS states section 1031 applies to real property held for business or investment, that a primary residence and property held mainly for sale do not qualify, and that exchanges involve a qualified intermediary, statutory deadlines, taxable boot and Form 8824.Decide in writing before any listing; a missed deadline cannot be repaired later.Verified ruleIRS — Like-kind exchanges (real estate tax tips) · IRS Publication 544 — Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets
What are the official inflation readings, and what do they not tell us?Read 2026-11-17: the BLS October 2026 CPI release reports a CPI-U index level of 337.511 and states that 12-month percent changes could not be published for most series because October 2025 data were not collected during the 2025 appropriations lapse.Quote the release date and its caveats; where official data are missing, widen the range instead of inventing a figure.Verified ruleBLS — CPI news release (October 2026) · U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — CPI
Is there an official long-term economic baseline to lean on?Read 2026-11-17: CBO's February 2026 outlook projects debt held by the public rising from 101 percent of GDP in 2026 to 120 percent in 2036 and PCE inflation returning to roughly the Federal Reserve's 2 percent goal in 2030, under current law.Use it as a bounded reference, not as a 20- or 50-year forecast for a household or a property.Verified ruleCBO — The Budget and Economic Outlook: 2026 to 2036 · Congressional Budget Office — Economic Outlook
