Taxes & projections

The property tax mechanics, and 40 years of them

California property tax is assessed value × tax rate, plus direct charges. Assessed value is the Prop 13 base value grown by an inflation factor that cannot exceed 2% a year — until a change of ownership resets it to market value. That reset is the whole inheritance question.

How the bill is built

  1. Start with the base year value

    Set at the last change of ownership (or 1975–76 roll value).

    Source: Santa Clara County Assessor — Understanding Prop 13.

  2. Grow it by the annual inflation factor, maximum 2%

    2026-27 factor: 1.02, the ninth consecutive year at the cap.

    Source: BOE Letter to Assessors 2026/002.

  3. Multiply by the tax rate area's rate

    1% constitutional levy plus voter-approved debt. Santa Clara County guidance: plan on 1.2%; San Francisco's FY 2026-27 secured rate is 1.18162182%.

    Sources: SCC Assessor FAQ, SF Treasurer.

  4. Add direct charges and subtract exemptions

    Parcel taxes and special assessments are flat or per-unit amounts, not percentages.

    Source: SF Treasurer — parcel tax exemptions.

What Prop 19 does to the Cupertino bill

If the transfer qualifies, the heir's taxable value is the parent's factored base plus any value above the indexed $1,000,000 cap. Because the base is only about $802,030 against a $5,959,750 value at 2045, most of the value is still reassessed — the exclusion is real but partial. If the transfer does not qualify, the entire market value is enrolled.

The eligibility trap

The exclusion requires the property to have been the transferor's principal residence, and then the transferee's principal residence within one year, with a homeowners' (or disabled veterans') exemption filed within that same year. Source: Santa Clara County Assessor, BOE LTA 2025/015. A trust alone does not create the exclusion, and renting the house out after moving in ends it.

Federal estate tax exposure over time

The federal exclusion is $15,000,000 for 2026 deaths and indexed thereafter. California has no estate or inheritance tax. Whatever the cash inheritance turns out to be ([AMOUNT REDACTED]), the only death-tax question is whether the total estate exceeds the indexed federal exclusion.
