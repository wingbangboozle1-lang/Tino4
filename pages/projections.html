A projection is a sentence about its assumptions

Before reading any year-by-year figure, read the assumption list that produced it. If a projection shows a single number for 2076, the honest reading is that it shows one path through a set of guesses. Two runs of the same model with different, equally plausible growth assumptions can reverse the ranking, and the useful output is the range and the assumption that flips it, not the centre line.

Avoid false precision

Publish ranges or dated official figures only. For every modeled amount, preserve the input source, observation date, formula, tax treatment, scenario range, and whether it is measured, assumed, or missing. Rebuild after any input change and record whether the ranking moved.

What this page publishes and what it does not

Property-specific amounts on this site are unit dollars: dollars per $1,000,000 of the household's 2026 baseline value. Multiply a unit figure by your own value divided by $1,000,000 to get your own dollars; the ranking does not change, because every line scales together. Amounts published by an official source are shown in their exact published dollars instead, and no household value, bill, rent or premium appears anywhere on the site. No assessed value, bill, rent or premium from the household's records is printed anywhere on this site: the workbook page shows the ratios those records imply, which is what the decision turns on. Model figures computed 2026-11-17 from labelled assumptions and the household's report, restated in unit dollars so no household figure is published; re-run with the owner's written records before relying on any of them.

How to read the projection

Columns two to four apply three growth assumptions to the household-reported value. Column five grows the current assessed value by the two percent statutory ceiling and stops there: while no change in ownership occurs, that is the taxable value. Columns seven and nine apply the two transfer branches at the modeled transfer year and then let the new base grow two percent a year. The Proposition 19 column keeps the factored base value and adds only the amount by which the value exceeds that base plus the indexed limit, which is why it lands between the other two - and why, with a base value near one sixth of the reported value, the claim helps without preserving today's bill. Ranges, not round-number points

Every ranking page publishes three value-growth scenarios, three portfolio-return scenarios, the low and high ends of the derived tax rate, the researched insurance range and a rent-yield band. A single point estimate is nowhere used as the basis for a recommendation. Re-run the model whenever an input changes and check whether the best path moved: that check is the deliverable, not the number.

Research framework only; not legal, tax, insurance, appraisal, investment, or property-management advice.

Recheck official authorities, source availability and law at the actual decision date.
