How to read the projection

Columns two to four apply three growth assumptions to the household-reported value. Column five grows the current assessed value by the two percent statutory ceiling and stops there: while no change in ownership occurs, that is the taxable value. Columns seven and nine apply the two transfer branches at the modeled transfer year and then let the new base grow two percent a year. The Proposition 19 column keeps the factored base value and adds only the amount by which the value exceeds that base plus the indexed limit, which is why it lands between the other two - and why, with a base value near one sixth of the reported value, the claim helps without preserving today's bill. Model figures computed 2026-11-17 from labelled assumptions and the household's rounded report; re-run with the owner's written records before relying on any of them.

Ranges, not round-number points

Every ranking page publishes three value-growth scenarios, three portfolio-return scenarios, the low and high ends of the derived tax rate, the researched insurance range and a rent-yield band. A single point estimate is nowhere used as the basis for a recommendation. Re-run the model whenever an input changes and check whether the best path moved: that check is the deliverable, not the number.
