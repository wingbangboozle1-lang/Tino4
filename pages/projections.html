Lay out the assumption gridCross the market-growth band with the inflation band and value each path at every cell, weighting the cells equally unless there is a documented reason not to.One rate implies precision the evidence cannot support; the grid is where a ranking is actually decided.GuidanceFederal Housing Finance Agency — House Price Index · U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — CPI
Publish the spread, not the middleReport the bottom, the quartiles, the median and the top of the ending values, in nominal and in inflation-adjusted terms, and keep the number of cells visible.A median hides the case where the plan fails, and the tails are what a household actually experiences.GuidanceSEC Investor.gov — Stocks · Congressional Budget Office — Long-Term Budget Outlook
Keep certainty on its own lineShow the property-tax consequence of the transfer separately from the growth assumption, because it does not depend on the market performing.A tax result that survives every cell of the grid is the only kind of figure a plan should lean on.GuidanceCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §51 · California BOE — Proposition 19

Avoid false precision

Publish ranges or dated official figures only. For every modeled amount, preserve the input source, observation date, formula, tax treatment, scenario range, and whether it is measured, assumed, or missing. Rebuild after any input change and record whether the ranking moved.

Research framework only; not legal, tax, insurance, appraisal, investment, or property-management advice.

Recheck official authorities, source availability and law at the actual decision date.
