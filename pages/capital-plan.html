Five-year maintenance and capital plan

A dated, item-by-item plan for both buildings, the reserve lines that fund them, the depreciation and recapture rules that decide what a holding path earns, and the annual re-run protocol.

Two buildings, two plans, one reserve discipline

Maintenance and capital cost is the line most likely to move a projected winner into a loss — and the line with no official source: no government publication prices a private roof, sewer lateral or turnover package. Each plan below is a scheduling and reserve tool with amount bands, not an estimate of this household's costs. Replace every band with a licensed bid before it enters a comparison.

Why both buildings get a plan, not just the inherited one

Property B is already owned and already tenanted, so its capital needs are present-day cash flows that compete with whatever the household does about Property A. A plan that prices only the inherited house implicitly assumes the other building costs nothing to hold, which is the assumption most likely to reverse a ranking.

Depreciation, recapture and the repair/improvement line

Three systems must not be mixed: the depreciation schedule drives income tax, the assessor's base year value drives property tax, and the estate appraisal drives basis. Each has its own source and its own clock.
