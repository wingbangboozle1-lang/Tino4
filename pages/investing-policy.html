Investing policy & liquidity

A documented framework for the sale proceeds, an estate liquidity plan, and the 1031 like-kind exchange decision — to be settled in writing before the money arrives.

Write the policy before the money arrives

A documented investing policy, an estate liquidity plan and an exchange decision should all be settled in writing before sale proceeds or the inheritance arrive. Choosing them after the money lands risks reverse-engineering the decision from one projection. This page provides the framework and the decision gates; a fee-only fiduciary and a CPA should finalize the actual policy.

When liquidity mattersIf the estate may approach or exceed the federal basic exclusion ($15,000,000 for 2026), the estate needs liquid assets to pay any estate tax when Form 706 is due nine months after death.Liquidity needs depend on the whole estate's composition and the year-of-death law; this is not a per-asset rule.VerifiedIRS — Estate tax · IRS — Estate-tax filing FAQs
Assets that provide liquidityCash, marketable securities and assets that can be sold or valued quickly can fund the tax; illiquid assets (real estate, closely-held business) may require special treatment.The liquidity mix is a household fact; confirm which assets are readily marketable.GuidanceIRS — Estate tax · SEC Investor.gov — Stocks FAQs

1031 like-kind exchange — decision framework

1031 like-kind exchange — decision framework
PointSource-backed ruleLimit / decision gateEvidence statusOfficial source / scope
What a 1031 exchange isA Section 1031 like-kind exchange defers gain when qualifying real property held for business or investment is exchanged for other qualifying real property; since 2017 it applies only to real property.Gain is deferred, not eliminated; the replacement property's basis carries over the relinquished property's basis with adjustments.VerifiedIRS Publication 544 — Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets
Property eligibilityBoth the relinquished and replacement property must be held for productive use in a trade or business or for investment; property held primarily for sale or for personal use does not qualify.Whether Property A (inherited, possibly to be occupied or sold) qualifies depends on its actual use and intent; a principal residence does not qualify.VerifiedIRS Publication 544 — Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets
Process and deadlinesA deferred exchange generally uses a qualified intermediary, requires identification of replacement property within 45 days and acquisition within 180 days, and must operate as an exchange rather than a sale followed by a purchase.Strict compliance is required; a disqualified intermediary or actual receipt of proceeds can convert the exchange into a taxable sale.VerifiedIRS Publication 544 — Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets
Is an exchange wanted?The decision depends on whether the household wants to defer gain and reinvest in another investment property, or take the cash. A principal-residence sale is not an exchange candidate.Take advice before selling; the decision interacts with the primary-residence exclusion, the income-tax layer and the liquidity plan.GuidanceIRS Publication 544 — Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets · IRS Topic no. 701 — Sale of your home
Advice before sellingBefore any sale, obtain written advice on whether a 1031 exchange is feasible and beneficial, and on the interaction with basis, depreciation recapture and the primary-residence exclusion.The exchange rules are technical; a tax adviser should review the specific facts before the sale is structured.Action neededIRS Publication 544 — Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets · IRS — Gifts and inheritances FAQ

Order of decisions

Settle the trust and title facts and the whole-estate position first, then the investing policy and liquidity plan, then the exchange question, and only then the entry-timing rule. Each later decision depends on the earlier ones; changing the order risks an answer that is reverse-engineered from a single projection.
