What the two parcels are, and what they cost today

Both properties sit on very low Prop 13 bases relative to their market value. That gap is the reason the inheritance decision matters: on the Cupertino house, the 2026 tax of about $7,340 is roughly 0.24% of what the house is worth.

The single most important number on this page

Cupertino assessed value is about 17.8% of market value. Everything the family can do between now and 2045 should be evaluated on whether it protects that gap — and the gap is protected only if the property stays in the family, is the parent's principal residence at death, and the heir occupies it.

What the history shows

  • Both assessments grew at almost exactly the 2% Prop 13 cap. The exceptions are 2021 (1% — the year the inflation factor was 1.01036) and 2016 (1.5%), consistent with BOE LTA 2026/002.
  • Cupertino's tax fell in 2017 while its assessment rose. Tax change −0.5% against assessed value +2.0%. That is consistent with a change in a tax-rate-area levy, not in the assessment, and is a reminder that the bill is rate × value + charges.
  • San Francisco's 2018 jump of 18.4% is far above the 2% assessment cap and roughly equal to 2% value growth plus new parcel charges. It has not been reconciled to a bill and is flagged.
  • Neither property has a homeowners' exemption recorded in the series. If either becomes the heir's principal residence, a $7,000 exemption applies — small in dollars (about $70–$80 a year) but it is also the filing that proves occupancy for Prop 19, which is worth far more.

Privacy handling

Personal information never leaves the working notes:

  • No street address, assessor's parcel number, block/lot, owner name, trustee name, beneficiary name or account number is stored in this repository or rendered on this site.
  • The two properties are identified only by ZIP code and county, which is the minimum needed to attach the correct tax rules.
  • The value that will be inherited as cash is carried as a redacted placeholder ([AMOUNT REDACTED]) — see Law Tracker for how the exemption applies to any amount.
  • Every figure that could identify a specific household (assessed values, tax bills) is presented as an index of the past, not linked to an address.
