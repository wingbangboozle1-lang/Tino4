Deadline timeline

The step-by-step inheritance process rendered as a deadline timeline: what happens at death, the one-year and three-year Proposition 19 windows, Form 706 timing, and the first-year operational checklist.

The inheritance sequence, step by step

A trust transfer avoids probate but not deadlines. The two clocks that decide the money are the one-year occupancy/exemption window and the three-year BOE-19-P claim window; the nine-month Form 706 clock decides the federal estate-tax side.

Deadline timeline

At death
The revocable trust becomes irrevocable for the deceased settlor's share; this is the change-in-ownership date that starts every clock below
Verified California BOE Property Tax Annotations 625.0000 · California Probate Code §16061.7
Within 60 days
Successor trustee serves the statutory notification on beneficiaries and heirs
Verified California Probate Code §16061.7
Within 150 days
Trustee files the change-in-ownership statement with the county recorder/assessor (Property A: Santa Clara County; Property B: San Francisco)
Verified California Revenue and Taxation Code §480
With recording
Preliminary Change of Ownership Report accompanies recorded documents; a $20 fee applies without it
Verified California Revenue and Taxation Code §480.3
Within 1 year — critical
If occupancy is intended: the Heir occupies Property A as a principal residence AND files the homeowners' exemption (BOE-266) within one year of the transfer date
Verified California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2 · California BOE Publication 801 — Proposition 19 fact sheet
As early as possible
File BOE-19-P with the county assessor; within three years for full relief, and the exclusion can be lost if the home transfers to a third party first
Verified California BOE Publication 801 — Proposition 19 fact sheet · Santa Clara County Assessor — transfers and Proposition 19
Month 1-2
Order date-of-death appraisals for both properties; document basis for the step-up and Form 706 schedules
Verified IRS Publication 551 — Basis of Assets · IRS Publication 559 — Survivors, Executors and Administrators
Within 9 months
If the gross estate exceeds the year-of-death threshold: file federal Form 706 (up to 6-month extension); a portability election also requires a timely filing
Verified IRS — Estate tax FAQs · IRS — Estate tax
California
No California estate tax return for deaths on or after 2005; no inheritance tax program for this generation
Verified California State Controller — Estate Tax
Dec 10 / Apr 10
Secured property-tax installments are delinquent after December 10 and April 10; supplemental bills for the year of transfer arrive separately
Verified San Francisco Treasurer & Tax Collector — property tax · Santa Clara County Assessor-Recorder
Ongoing year 1
Insurance: notify carriers immediately; vacancy clauses can strip coverage on an unoccupied home; obtain quotes before the transfer if a sale or rental is possible
Guidance California Department of Insurance — Residential insurance · California FAIR Plan Association
Ongoing year 1
Utilities and solar: transfer water/electric accounts; confirm the solar tariff's transferability (NEM agreements do not automatically follow a new owner)
Guidance California Public Utilities Commission — Net energy metering and net billing · California State Water Resources Control Board — Drinking Water Program · San Francisco Public Utility Commission
If selling
A prompt sale after death realizes gain against a stepped-up basis (near zero in the base case); California withholding applies only if the seller is a nonresident
Verified IRS Publication 551 — Basis of Assets · California Revenue and Taxation Code §18662
If renting
Reassessment applies because the occupancy test failed; leases, rent-cap rules and landlord coverage change the cost structure
Verified California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2 · California Civil Code §1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act) · California Department of Insurance — Residential insurance
Homeowners' exemption caveat
The $7,000 exemption does not extend to rented or vacant property — the occupancy intent and this filing decide the Prop 19 exclusion together
Verified California Revenue and Taxation Code §218 · California Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2

Who does what

The successor trustee executes the notifications and filings; the estate attorney confirms the trust interpretation and any Form 706 strategy; the CPA handles fiduciary returns and basis reporting; the insurance professional reprices both properties for their next use; the family decides occupancy. The one-page attorney and CPA brief collects the decisions that need their signatures.
