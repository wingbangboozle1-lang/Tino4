LAW & PROCESS

Inheritance process and deadline timeline

A relative step-by-step sequence for a trust-based transfer, with official clocks distinguished from operational checkpoints.

Start clocks only from their legal trigger

These deadlines are not interchangeable. A trust-notice clock, county property-tax filing, federal estate return and Proposition 19 claims can have different triggers and responsible filers. Counsel should confirm the actual dates from the death and transfer records. The owner-planned transfer window is 2040–2050 — a planning assumption, not a confirmed date.

Step-by-step sequence

  1. Death of the transferor — the revocable trust becomes irrevocable and every clock below starts (owner-planned window: 2040–2050, planning assumption only).
  2. Within 60 days — trustee serves the notification on beneficiaries and heirs (Probate Code §16061.7).
  3. Within 150 days — change-in-ownership statement filed with the county recorder or assessor for the trust transfer (Revenue and Taxation Code §480).
  4. Within 1 year — transferee occupies the inherited house as a principal residence and files the homeowners' exemption — the first Proposition 19 claim step.
  5. Within 9 months (extendable by 6) — Form 706 filed if the estate meets the filing test or a portability election is needed.
  6. Within 3 years — Proposition 19 exclusion claim filed, or earlier if occupancy ends or the property passes to a third party.
  7. Every fiscal year — secured property-tax installments (due November 1 and February 1; delinquent December 10 and April 10) and Form 1041 if the estate or trust has income.
  8. Before any occupancy, rental or sale — insurance bound, legal use and leases verified, tax reserves set, written path approval from the household and advisers.
  9. After the date-of-death basis attaches — sell the inherited house or occupy it and complete the exclusion filings; a sale before death would not receive the step-up.
  10. 24–36 months after the proceeds arrive — park in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries while phasing into equities; re-price yields at each auction.
  11. Annually thereafter — refresh law, assessments, insurance, rents, yields and the suggestion review queue.
