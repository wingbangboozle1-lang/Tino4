Step-by-step inheritance process

The California trust sequence with every statutory deadline, from trustee notification to the three routes for the house.

Privacy & verification controls. The two properties appear only as Property A (the trust-held residence to be inherited) and Property B (the already-owned 3-unit building). No name, street address, parcel or account number, city name, neighbourhood name or ZIP code is published. Every owner-supplied figure was rounded to the nearest $10 before modelling, so exact tax-roll values cannot be matched by search. Official cells that no government source supports are left blank and explained on the Flagged for review page.

The sequence below is the California trust path, with the statutory deadline that drives each step. Deadlines run from the date of death, not from the date a beneficiary learns of it.

Days 1–30Obtain certified death certificates, locate the signed trust and any amendments, obtain a separate tax identification number for the trust, and confirm the property stays insured in the trust's name.A coverage gap or frozen accounts; the trust cannot sell what it cannot insure or transact on.S01 S20 S36
Within 60 days — trustee notificationServe the statutory notification on every beneficiary and every heir at law.The contest period does not start on schedule and the trustee is exposed to surcharge.S27
Months 1–4 — date-of-death appraisalCommission a date-of-death appraisal of Property A and inventory all financial assets; this document is the basis evidence for every later tax filing.Unsupported basis on the final return and on any depreciation schedule if the house is rented.S07 S15 S28
Within 150 days — change in ownership reportFile the death-of-owner change-in-ownership statement with the county assessor, even if the property will be sold.A statutory penalty computed on the reassessed tax.S26 S28
Within 9 months — federal estate returnFile Form 706 if the worldwide estate plus prior taxable gifts exceeds the year-of-death exclusion, or to elect portability for a surviving spouse. A six-month filing extension is available, but tax is still due at nine months.Late-filing and late-payment penalties, or loss of a multi-million-dollar portable exclusion.S01 S02
Annual — fiduciary income taxFile the federal fiduciary return and the California fiduciary return for income earned between death and distribution, with the beneficiary statements.Trust-level brackets compress quickly: the top capital-gain rate applies at a low income threshold for trusts.S03 S11 S20
Route 1 — sell (recommended)Sell from the administrative trust or immediately after distribution; record the trustee's death affidavit and the preliminary change of ownership report; pay 0.11% county transfer tax and the prorated supplemental bill; deploy the proceeds on a staged schedule.Delay converts post-death appreciation into taxable gain and keeps the reassessed carrying cost running.S07 S15 S18 S28 S29
Route 2 — occupy under Prop. 19Move in as principal residence within 1 year of death, file the $7,000 exemption claim within 1 year (relinquishing it on Property B), and file the parent-child exclusion claim within 3 years.Losing the 1-year window forfeits retroactive relief; missing the 3-year claim limits relief to the future.S22 S23 S24
Route 3 — rent it outComplete the same ownership reports, then place the property in service, start depreciation on the building share only, and keep contemporaneous records of every repair and improvement.No parent-child relief at all, and depreciation errors surface as recapture on the eventual sale.S12 S22 S21
