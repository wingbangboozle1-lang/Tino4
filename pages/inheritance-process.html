Inheritance process and deadline timeline

A visual timeline of the statutory deadlines and a ten-step walkthrough of the trust-based transfer — from the day of death through implementing the chosen path.

Start each clock from its legal trigger

The trust-notice clock, county property-tax filing, federal estate return and Proposition 19 claims have different triggers and responsible filers. Counsel should confirm actual dates from the death and transfer records; the timeline below shows relative deadlines only.

  1. Day 0Death / trust irrevocable
  2. Day 60Trustee notice (§16061.7)
  3. Day 150Change-in-ownership statement (RTC §480)
  4. Month 9Form 706 if required (+6 mo via Form 4768)
  5. Month 12Move in + homeowners’ exemption (Prop 19)
  6. Year 3Exclusion claim deadline (or before 3rd-party transfer)
  7. OngoingAnnual bills, Form 1041 if needed, law re-checks

Step 1 — Death / trust becomes irrevocableDay 0The trust instrument governs who acts and when. Collect the executed trust, amendments, deed and trustee contacts immediately.GuidanceCalifornia Probate Code §16061.7
Step 2 — Trustee notice to beneficiariesBy day 60Probate Code §16061.7 requires the statutory notice in specified events, including when a revocable trust becomes irrevocable at death. Serve and document.GuidanceCalifornia Probate Code §16061.7
Step 3 — Change-in-ownership statementBy day 150RTC §480 requires the statement for specified real-property transfers by reason of death, including through a trust. File with the county and keep proof; ask the assessor about any Proposition 19 claim packet.GuidanceCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §480
Step 4 — Valuation and basis recordsMonths 1–9Obtain date-of-death appraisals for every property; the executor's basis statement (Form 8971/Schedule A when required) fixes the income-tax basis for the heirs.GuidanceIRS — Gifts and inheritances FAQ
Step 5 — Estate inventory and Form 706 decisionBy month 9Compute gross estate + adjusted taxable gifts against the year-of-death exclusion. If required (or for portability), Form 706 is due 9 months after death; Form 4768 buys up to 6 more months. Tax payment clocks are separate.GuidanceIRS — Estate-tax filing FAQs
Step 6 — Decide the Property A pathMonths 1–12The occupancy decision must be made early: the family-home exclusion needs the transferee's principal residence within 1 year plus the homeowners' exemption filing. Trustee authority to occupy must exist.GuidanceCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19
Step 7 — File the exclusion claimBy year 3 (or before any third-party transfer)BOE guidance: the intergenerational exclusion claim is generally filed within 3 years of transfer or before a transfer to a third party. Calendar it alongside the 1-year occupancy/exemption deadline — they are separate clocks.GuidanceCalifornia BOE — Proposition 19
Step 8 — Insurance, utilities and tenant setupBefore occupancy or rentalBind use-appropriate insurance on both properties, transfer utilities, confirm legal units and leases on Property B, and reserve taxes and repairs before taking on rental risk.GuidanceCalifornia Department of Insurance — Residential insurance
Step 9 — Trust income reportingAnnually, if requiredA fiduciary may need Form 1041 for trust/estate income. Retained rental income above the low trust threshold is taxed at 37% (2026) — distribute or distribute the asset.GuidanceIRS — About Form 1041
Step 10 — Implement the chosen pathAfter the gates closeOccupy A (Path 1) or list/sell A and begin the 24–36 month Treasury staging plan (Path 2), keeping B rented. Re-check insurance and law annually.GuidanceSEC Investor.gov — Stocks FAQs
