What this page is

Every headline figure this project relies on, with the date it was read from the issuing authority and the exact words that establish it. A figure that could not be confirmed is left blank and marked, rather than estimated. Re-reading these is the first task of the annual review, because an amount that was right in 2026 is not evidence about 2045.

What is deliberately blank

No figure is published for a specific parcel's assessed value, tax-rate area, levy, insurance premium, utility bill, rent, repair cost or sale price. Those are obtainable only from the property's own records and the issuing agency, and publishing them would breach the privacy boundary. The Projection worksheet lists exactly how each one is obtained and what it is cross-checked against.

How to re-verify

Open each link, confirm the amount is still current for the relevant year, and record the date. If an amount has been superseded, update the row and the date rather than keeping a stale figure that a later reader would take as current. Numbers published for a future year by an agency are recorded as published for that year only and are never extrapolated.

A correction made on 2026-11-17

Two citations on this site were corrected after reading the sources. The 57% S&P 500 figure is in the 'Great Recession' essay, not the 'Great Recession and Its Aftermath' essay, and the two give different home-price windows (about 30% from mid-2006 to mid-2009, versus over one fifth from 2007Q1 to 2011Q2). Both are now cited to the essay that actually contains them. Separately, a formula error that left reserve interest permanently undeployed in the staging model was found and fixed by a new test.
