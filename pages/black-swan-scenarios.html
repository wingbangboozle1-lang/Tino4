50-year black-swan economic conditions

Seven non-uniform regimes — stagflation, crisis at the transfer date, sequence-of-returns trap, seismic and insurance shock, basis repeal, equity lost decade.

Privacy & verification controls. The two properties appear only as Property A (the trust-held residence to be inherited) and Property B (the already-owned 3-unit building). No name, street address, parcel or account number, city name, neighbourhood name or ZIP code is published. Every owner-supplied figure was rounded to the nearest $10 before modelling, so exact tax-roll values cannot be matched by search. Official cells that no government source supports are left blank and explained on the Flagged for review page.

A 50-year projection that grows at a constant rate is a ruler, not a forecast. This separate section replays seven non-uniform histories — real inflation spikes, a financial crisis that lands on the transfer year, a crash immediately after the sale, a seismic and insurance shock, a repeal of stepped-up basis, and a long equity stagnation with a local housing boom — against the smooth baseline.

What the stress tests change, and what they do not

FindingConsequence for the planOfficial basis
Selling survives every regimePlan 1A or 1C is the top or near-top plan in all seven; no regime makes holding the house the best financial choice, although renting nearly closes the gap when equities stagnate.S42 S44
The mix matters more than the decisionIn the stagnating-equity regime a 25% Treasury sleeve, or even 100% Treasuries, beats 100% equities — which is the argument for staging, not for holding the house.S18 S40
The seizure risk is statutory, not marketA repeal of IRC §1014 would tax the pre-death gain and is the one change that materially weakens selling; it is tracked on the law watch page.S07 S46
Insurance is the underestimated shockA seismic event with a 45% premium jump and an uninsured repair bill hits whichever property is held; holding two concentrates that exposure.S36 S37 S25
