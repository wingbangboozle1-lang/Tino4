Black-swan economic conditions

Growth and inflation are never steady for 50 years. This page re-runs the plans through the real record instead of a smooth line.

Why this page exists: the projections elsewhere use one steady rate for each thing. Real economies do not behave like that. Here the same plans are pushed through the actual year-by-year record from 1977 to 2025 — inflation, local house prices, rents, interest rates and stocks, all moving together as they really did.

Result: with the proceeds in stocks, selling was the best plan in 96% of 49 replays. With the proceeds in Treasuries only, selling was best in just 12%: historically, local house prices outran bond interest.

The one hard start where keeping the house beat selling into stocks was 2000–2019: stocks lost three years running at the outset while local house prices kept climbing. That is the real risk in the recommended plan — a market fall soon after investing a lump sum. Investing in stages over a year or two, or holding part in Treasuries, reduces it.

What the stress tests change in the plan

  • Sell, but invest for growth. Selling and then holding savings or bonds gives up the advantage; in most of history that was worse than keeping the house as a rental.
  • Spread the entry. The worst outcomes come from a market fall right after investing everything at once.
  • Keep Property B through all of it. Its rents and its capped tax bill held up in every sequence, which is what lets the household ride out bad years.
  • Living in Property A was never the best financial result in any replay — it is a lifestyle choice with a price that varies with the economy.

Limits

  • 49 years of data, one country, one metro area. The future can be worse than anything in the record.
  • Stock returns are computed from year-end index levels and dividend yields in official tables; they match published index-fund returns to within about half a point a year.
  • Treasuries are modelled as earning each year's yield, without the price swings of a bond fund.
  • Tax law is held at today's rules, with brackets rising with each path's inflation.
