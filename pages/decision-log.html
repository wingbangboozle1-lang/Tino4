Decision log and review queue

Twelve suggestions for improving decision quality, each with its effort, privacy impact and a local-only review choice. Nothing is applied automatically.

A review queue, not an automatic change

Each suggestion below records the decision quality it improves, the effort, and its privacy impact. Choosing an option changes nothing on the site and sends nothing anywhere; it only records your own review state in this browser. Implement a change only after the relevant owner has reviewed it and the necessary private records exist.

D13 Correct the Proposition 19 value-limit modelEarlier pages modeled the qualifying branch as keeping the factored base year value alone and the non-qualifying branch as market value less the limit. Read against RTC §63.2 and BOE Letter to Assessors 2026/026, the correct pair is: qualifying base = factored base year value plus the excess of full cash value over (that base + the adjusted limit); non-qualifying base = full cash value at the transfer. The model, both page tables and the browser tool now use the corrected pair everywhere.The value limit is the binding constraint on a high-value home, so the correction lowers the modeled benefit of qualifying and raises the modeled cost of failing to. Leaving it in place would have made the occupancy decision look roughly twice as valuable as the statute allows, which is a decision-changing error rather than a rounding difference.Decision quality: High · Effort: Medium · Privacy impact: NoneGuidanceCalifornia Revenue and Taxation Code §63.2 · California BOE — Letter to Assessors 2026/026 (Proposition 19) · California BOE — Proposition 19
D14 Reclassify San Francisco Proposition H from pending research to enacted taxProposition H was carried in earlier sessions as a possibly-pending tenant/rent measure. It is an approved 15-year Muni parcel tax on owners (November 3, 2026), billed with the property tax from fiscal 2027-28 through 2041-42, with published single-family, multifamily, commercial and vacant-land rates, an inflation index, a seniors application, and a limited 50% pass-through for rent-controlled owners.A recurring line-item tax on the owned building belongs in the carrying stack of every path, and a rent-control assumption that does not exist must not be imported into the tenant analysis. The election result is published as preliminary and flagged as uncertified.Decision quality: High · Effort: Low · Privacy impact: NoneGuidanceSFMTA — Muni Proposition H parcel tax · San Francisco Elections — November 3, 2026 results · San Francisco Rent Board — Department page
D15 Publish market rent on Property A as a floor, not an estimateThe only available rent evidence for the area is third-party listing aggregates, so the rent path is stated as: gross rent must clear the modeled break-even, and any conclusion that renting wins is provisional until a written comparable study exists. No rent figure on this site is labeled as an appraisal of this house.Listing aggregates are the weakest input in the file and the one most likely to be mistaken for knowledge. Declaring them a floor keeps the honest asymmetry: the tax branch is derived from statute, the rent branch is not.Decision quality: High · Effort: Low · Privacy impact: NoneGuidanceOwner-supplied records — Property A (privacy-rounded to nearest $10) · HUD — Fair Market Rents · IRS Publication 527 — Residential Rental Property
D16 Model a sale in the same tax year as the transfer firstThe sale-timing page puts the transfer-year sale ahead of every later exit case, and the depreciation, section 121 and recapture consequences are modeled as the cost of waiting rather than as separate topics.The stepped-up basis makes the transfer-year answer close to settled and makes every later year a quantifiable cost; leading with the later cases buried the only comparison the owner can act on now.Decision quality: High · Effort: Medium · Privacy impact: NoneGuidanceIRS — Gifts and inheritances FAQ · IRS — Instructions for Schedule D (Form 1041) · IRS Tax Topic 409 — Capital gain and loss · California FTB — Capital gains and losses
D17 Leave a public tax rate area number unpublished by designThe property-tax page explains how to read the rate area off the bill and refuses to print the number or to treat the county's 1.1488 worked example as Property A's rate; the residual difference in the supplied figures is shown as an unresolved discrepancy instead of being assigned to a special district.A tax rate area number identifies the parcel, and guessing what the residual on a bill represents would manufacture a fact. Both choices keep the model honest in the same direction.Privacy: High · Decision quality: Medium · Privacy impact: ProtectiveGuidanceSanta Clara County — Compilation of Tax Rates FY 2025-26 · Santa Clara County Assessor — 2026 Assessment Information booklet · Owner-supplied records — Property A (privacy-rounded to nearest $10)
