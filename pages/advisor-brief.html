PRIVATE WORKING SESSION · ATTORNEY / CPA

Two-property inheritance decision brief

Public copy intentionally omits addresses, personal balances and property-specific dollar totals. Fill case-specific amounts only in the advisers’ private workpapers.

Case frame

  • Property A: expected to pass through a trust in the 2040–2050 window; transfer date, debt, title and trust terms are unverified.
  • Property B: already owned by the inheritor, multi-unit and partly rented; analyze its actual tenancy and cash flow separately.
  • Do not treat a projected inheritance date, future law, estimated rent or modelled market return as a verified fact.

Decision gates

  • Can A legally and practically be occupied, and does occupancy meet current transfer-tax requirements?
  • What are the estate-wide tax result, date-of-death basis, liabilities, insurance and actual net rental economics?
  • How much sequence loss can be tolerated without a forced sale? Keep reinvestment risk beside the sale headline.

Attorney / trustee

Review the executed trust and amendments, deed/title, authority, debt, beneficiary rights, Prop. 19 eligibility, tenant rights, and exact filing triggers. Provide a written deadline calendar.

CPA

Build an estate-wide inventory; assess any Form 706 and fiduciary returns; confirm basis and depreciation; compare occupancy, rent and sale proceeds after tax; stress-test liquidity and tax assumptions.

Property / insurance advisers

Verify actual leases and lawful unit use; obtain comparable rent opinions, inspections, utility/repair history, and matched owner-occupied, landlord and earthquake coverage quotes.

Next review: at the actual death/vesting or distribution event, before any occupancy, lease, deed transfer, sale or investment decision. See the full deadline timeline and the entry-risk lab. This is an issue-spotting sheet, not legal, tax or investment advice.
