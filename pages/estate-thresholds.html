Estate tax, gift exclusion and headroom

Federal thresholds currently verified from IRS sources, California death-tax law, and the specific facts still required to determine estate exposure.

The threshold is not per heir

Federal estate tax is an estate-level transfer tax. The IRS filing test uses the decedent's gross estate, adjusted taxable gifts and specific gift-tax exemption; a portability election can require a return below the threshold. A recipient's share alone cannot establish whether a return is required or tax is due.

Liquidity, gifts and portability — the estate facts this site cannot see

Every row is blank by policy. A property-level model can say whether a threshold is approached; only an estate inventory can say whether the estate can meet a filing, make an election, and pay without forcing the sale of the very house the plan is trying to keep. This is the point at which 'maximize profit, minimize cost' stops being a property question and becomes an estate-liquidity question.

Cash in the estate, stated plainly

Two houses worth a combined rounded value of about $4,900,000 at today's owner-supplied estimates can sit well under a federal filing threshold while still being unable to pay a filing fee, a repair, an insurance premium and a tax bill in the same ninety days. Estate tax is paid in cash on a fixed date, and real property is not cash. The plan's protection against a forced sale is not the exclusion amount — it is liquid reserves sized against the executor's calendar, which is why the four rows above are treated as prerequisites rather than refinements. The site publishes no reserve figure, because the household's cash position is not published information.

Annex: the comparison premises

A threshold comparison is only usable if five things are stated on the same line: level (estate-wide, not one asset), direction (a tax or an exclusion), unit (per estate, per person, per heir), year of death, and baseline (current law only). Anything else is a headline, not a calculation.

Future law and the household calculation

The transfer is decades away. The 2030-2050 rows above are an assumption path, not a forecast: Congress can change the exclusion, and the actual chained-CPI adjustments will differ. For the household number, the CPA combines the gross estate (Property A, Property B, cash and other assets), adjusted taxable gifts, deductions and any portability facts in the year of death. California imposes no state estate or inheritance tax under current law (RTC §13301). Annual gifts use the separate annual exclusion: $19,000 per donor/recipient in 2026 and $20,000 in 2027.
