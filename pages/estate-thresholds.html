Estate tax & headroom

A clear distinction between current law, missing evidence and illustrative scenarios.

Tax is not a penalty for inheriting

Federal estate tax is generally an obligation of the decedent’s estate, not a separate tax on each beneficiary’s inherited principal. Filing tests, taxable estate, available credits and income tax are distinct. This brief assumes the U.S.-citizen/resident estate framework only for the statutory examples; actual domicile/citizenship must be confirmed.

Estate tax & headroom

Current law, owner figures, stated assumptions and open questions, kept apart on purpose.

There is no inheritance tax to plan around, and that is the good news

California prohibits an inheritance or estate tax of its own, and the state controller confirms no California estate return is required for anyone dying after 2004. The federal estate tax is a tax on the estate above the exclusion for the year of death, not on what any one heir receives. On current law an estate well past $15,480,000 is needed before any federal tax is due, and the money mentioned in the brief would have to be tested against the whole estate, which is not known.

The real risk is legislative, not arithmetic

A future Congress can lower the exclusion, and it has changed several times since 2001. The 2027 figure is indexed, so on unchanged law it rises above $15,480,000. The two things that would actually matter are a reduction in the exclusion and a repeal of the step-up in basis. Neither is enacted, and the one bill this session checked that would repeal the step-up was introduced and referred to committee, not passed.
