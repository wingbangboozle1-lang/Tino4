Insurance: what can and cannot be verified

The California Department of Insurance publishes policy forms, coverage guidance and complaint data, but no official premium for a given ZIP code or parcel. Premiums are individually rated on dwelling value, roof age, claims history and fire-score. The $1,500 figure for each property is therefore an owner estimate and is flagged as unverified.

  • CDI homeowners guide — coverage and shopping guidance (verified).
  • California FAIR Plan — insurer of last resort. The FAIR Plan writes fire-only dwelling coverage; a separate difference-in-conditions policy is normally needed for full HO-3 coverage, so a FAIR Plan quote is not a like-for-like replacement for a $1,500 homeowners policy.
Planning caution

A three-unit 1920s building in the Sunset with two tenancies is a landlord policy, not a homeowners policy, and a Cupertino house with solar and a $3.1M replacement cost is unlikely to be insured for $1,500 at today's construction costs. Both figures are carried as reported because no official source exists, but the model's 5%/yr growth from a $1,500 base is more likely to understate than overstate. Resolve this with two actual quotes before relying on the cash-flow projections.

Water and power

  • San Francisco water. SFPUC's residential volumetric rate was $11.40 per unit (748 gallons) for the first four units a month in FY 2025-26, proposed to rise to $12.20 in FY 2026-27. The average single-family combined water-and-sewer bill is about $171 a month. Three units at $330–$400 a month is consistent with that average once sewer and common-area use are included — the owner-reported figure passes a sanity check. SFPUC Prop 218 notice.
  • Cupertino water. Supplied by San Jose Water, whose tariff is filed with the CPUC. The published tariff could not be retrieved in this window, so $100–$150 a month stands as owner-reported only.
  • Electricity. PG&E Schedule E-1 is the applicable residential tariff (PG&E E-1). The Cupertino house is net-negative because of rooftop solar; the model credits $180 a year and grows the credit at 4%. The San Francisco building's $30–$70 a month is common-area power only if tenants pay their own accounts; if the owner pays all three units, this line is understated.

Why maintenance is a percentage, not a guess

There is no official source for "maintenance on a Bay Area house", so the model anchors to replacement-cost logic: roughly 0.2% of value a year for a single house with solar and recent systems, and 0.5% for a three-unit building. Deferred maintenance is the risk: a roof, a sewer lateral or a foundation on a 1920s Sunset building can be a $50,000–$100,000 event that averages out to far more in the year it happens. The Scenario Analysis does not model a capital event; treat the 0.5% as a floor.
