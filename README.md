# Privacy-first inheritance decision guide

This repository builds a public, source-linked guide to inheritance planning, property-tax rules, rental and sale considerations, investment-sequence risk, and adviser questions. It publishes a **rounded** scenario model so the arithmetic can be checked line by line, while deliberately publishing **no** property location, address, parcel identifier, account number, lease text, insurance declaration, identity, or unrounded household figure.

## Rounded-figure publication policy — highest priority

- Every dollar amount generated by the model is rounded to the nearest ten dollars before it reaches a page, so a published value cannot be matched to a public record by an exact search and so the tables stay checkable by hand. `tools/verify.py` fails the build on any currency amount that is not a multiple of ten, unless it is an amount published by an official source (listed in `OFFICIAL_AMOUNTS`).
- Identifiers never enter the model or the site: locality, address, parcel, account, policy, and party names belong in a private workpaper. The generator embeds no data file and reads none; the verifier also fails the build on postal-like, phone, email, and street patterns, and on non-visible attributes or HTML comments.
- Exact and unrounded records live in `private-work/` and `private-data/`, both git-ignored, plus the owner's encrypted offline store. Nothing in either directory may be copied into a page, a commit message, a pull-request body, or an issue comment.
- Assumption rates are labelled as assumptions wherever they print (value growth, cost inflation, gross rent yield, portfolio return, tax drag, reserve yield). An item no official source supports is flagged on the "Flagged for review" page with the specific act that would settle it; it never becomes a headline figure.
- Keep deeds, trusts, tax returns, parcel identifiers, account numbers, leases, insurance declarations, utility bills, appraisals, quotes and adviser correspondence outside this repository.
- Browser what-if tools run in memory only and round their own output to the nearest ten dollars. The site has no remote scripts, forms, analytics, network calls, or browser storage; refresh clears entered values.
- A working-tree cleanup cannot erase earlier public commits, pull-request diffs, forks, caches, downloads, or clones, which contain drafts from before this policy. The historical exposure remains unresolved and must be treated as potentially retained. Repository administrators should assess visibility and history remediation; preserve a private encrypted archive before any destructive history action.
- Public GitHub Pages and a private family case file are different products. Do not place confidential case records in the public site or repository.

## Site map

The 22 generated pages group the work into executive/decision summaries, the full requirements tracker, private inventory and cost checklists, property-tax and estate law, deadlines, black-swan conditions, Treasury staging, a draft investment policy, adviser handoff, questions, suggestions, a decision log, a glossary, source register, privacy limits and flagged items.

The homepage puts **what would change the answer** on the first screen, immediately above the modeled tax step-up a transfer causes. The "Rounded figure workbook" page carries the reported bill histories and the derived rates; "Compare paths" ranks eight pure and mixed paths — sell at transfer, sell then park 24–36 months in short Treasuries, sell with monthly tranches, rent-then-sell, live-then-sell, hold-and-rent, live with and without a valid exclusion claim — at identical transfer dates (2040, 2045, 2050) and horizons (2055, 2070), with taxes, debt, occupancy, capital costs, liquidity and reinvestment risk treated explicitly. The ranking is reported as the arithmetic of labelled assumptions: at the baseline it favors selling in the transfer year in all three growth scenarios, and at the soft portfolio scenario it inverts, which is stated on the same page rather than buried. Nothing here is a recommendation for a household; every row either cites an official source for the rule it applies or is flagged as unresolved.

## Build and verify

```sh
python3 tools/build.py
python3 tools/verify.py
python3 -m unittest discover -s tools -p 'test_*.py' -v
```

CI rebuilds and verifies the generated site. The verifier checks the page manifest, source and evidence badge on every table row, source-host allowlist, local links, CSP/noindex, that every published currency amount is a multiple of ten dollars (or an official published amount), and that the builder reads no external file. `tools/test_model.py` additionally pins the rounding rule, the rate derivation from the reported bills, the Proposition 19 value formula, the proceeds waterfall identity, the staging arithmetic, byte-for-byte rebuild reproducibility, and the identifier scan.

## Evidence standard

Official sources establish only the narrow rule or public information stated beside each link. They do not verify private title, eligibility, assessment, valuation, tax basis, rental legality, costs or insurance coverage. Missing and unverified items are labeled and given a next step. Future laws, economic paths, insurance costs, rents and returns are not presented as official forecasts.

## Important limits

This is general research, not legal, tax, appraisal, insurance, investment or property-management advice. Recheck current law and official deadlines with qualified advisers at the actual transfer date. Use a private, access-controlled, offline model for household figures, after-tax scenarios and final decisions.
