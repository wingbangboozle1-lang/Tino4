Best plan and why

The ranked recommendation, the five mechanisms behind it, what executing it involves, and the conditions that would change the answer.

Privacy & verification controls. The two properties appear only as Property A (the trust-held residence to be inherited) and Property B (the already-owned 3-unit building). Official cells that no government source supports are left blank and explained on the Flagged for review page.
Bottom line. Sell Property A when the trust distributes it, stay in Property B unit 3, and reinvest in a staged equity portfolio with a Treasury reserve. The plan wins on every transfer year from 2040 to 2050 in the baseline, and it wins because of structural arithmetic — a tax-free basis reset, a property-tax base that resets upward whether you occupy or rent, and a rental yield below every liquid alternative — not because of a lucky return assumption.

What the winning plan actually involves

StepActionWhy it matters hereOfficial basis
Year 0 — months 1–6 after deathAppraise Property A at the date of death, open the trust's own tax identity, and sell from the administrative trust or immediately after distribution.Locks the basis that makes the sale tax-free, and stops post-death appreciation from becoming taxable gain.S07 S15 S27
Year 0 — salePay 5.00% of proceeds in brokerage/closing costs and 0.11% county transfer tax; expect a prorated supplemental property-tax bill from the reassessment date.The only material friction in the plan, and it is small relative to one year of reassessed carrying cost.S29 S28
Years 0–3Move the proceeds into a broad equity index in 24–36 monthly tranches, holding the uninvested balance in Treasury bills while the schedule runs.Removes the single-day entry risk that regime R3 punishes; Treasury interest is exempt from California income tax.S18 S43
OngoingKeep Property B's $7,000 homeowners' exemption, claim the 1.6% allowable increase each year, and hold a maintenance reserve.Protects the one asset whose property-tax base is permanently low.S24 S31
