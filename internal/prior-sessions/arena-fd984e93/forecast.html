Important distinction: market price and assessed value are different. Proposition 13's annual cap applies to assessed value, not the home's market value; a change in ownership can establish a new base-year value. A qualifying Prop 19 exclusion can change that outcome, but 2040–2050 eligibility and exclusion amount are not known today.

Recommended model once inputs exist

  1. Year-of-transfer appraised market value and county enrolled assessment, separately.
  2. Multiple value scenarios only when expressly labeled as assumptions (downside/base/upside), not asserted as facts.
  3. Parcel-specific tax schedules under both ordinary reassessment and an assessor-confirmed Prop 19 result.
  4. Written insurer quotes and utility tariffs/actual usage with explicit escalation scenarios.
  5. Property inspection-based annual maintenance and separately scheduled capital replacements.
  6. After-tax rent, sale proceeds, or avoided housing costs vs reinvestment opportunity using dated and clearly identified assumptions.

Legal-change watch

As of this review, California SB 293 (2025) has been enacted (Chapter 539, Oct. 10, 2025) and amended Revenue and Taxation Code sections 63.1/63.2 concerning intergenerational exclusions. This is a change to current law, not a guarantee that the same rules will remain in force by 2040–2050. No unpassed bill is treated as current law. Recheck official legislative status and the BOE's current claim rules at transfer.

San Francisco 2026 ballot: preliminary results posted Nov. 4 show Measure I (“Changes to Real Property Transfer Tax”) failed, so its proposed dedication of a portion of certain high-value transfer-tax revenue is not treated as law here. Measure J passed and changes a foreclosure exemption beginning March 1, 2027 for specified properties; this concerns foreclosure transfers, not an ordinary voluntary sale. The results are preliminary, and the enacted ordinance / certified results should be checked before relying on it.
