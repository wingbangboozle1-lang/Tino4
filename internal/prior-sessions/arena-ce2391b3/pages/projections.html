Projections

Market value, assessed value, property tax, rent and cash flow, year by year, with the basis of every column stated.

How the projection works

Property tax

Assessed value rises at most 2% a year on the existing roll — the BOE set the 2026-27 inflation factor at exactly 1.02 California State Board of Equalization. Tax is assessed value times the property's rate. Property A's rate is the 1.3358% implied by its own bills; Property B's is the 2.1357% implied by its own. Both are flagged, because they differ from the published county rates.

Market value

Grows at 3.0% a year for Property A and 3.0% for Property B. The anchor is the FHFA all-transactions California HPI, which compounded at about 3.8% from 2004 to 2026 Federal Housing Finance Agency — the base case sits deliberately below that because the index barely moved in the last year (+0.90%) and Cupertino's population fell 3.2% between 2020 and 2025 U.S. Census Bureau.

Rent

Property B's $7,150/month grows at 2.0% a year. That is an assumption, not an entitlement: San Francisco's allowable increase is 1.6% for 2026–27 City and County of San Francisco, and the statewide cap under AB 1482 disappears on January 1, 2030 as written California Legislative Information.

What is deliberately not in this table

Market value is not the same as assessed value and the gap only widens. That gap is the whole reason Property A is worth less to hold than to sell — a buyer would pay 1.3358% on $3,100,000, not on $550,540.

Cash flow under each strategy

Net cash is rent collected, less vacancy, management, both properties' operating costs and property tax, less income tax on net rental income. Negative means the properties require money out of pocket. Property B alone, net of its own costs, is negative in the live-in case — the rent does not cover the building's tax, water, insurance and reserve on the stated figures.
