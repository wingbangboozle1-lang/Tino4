Utilities: what the tariffs actually say

Property A — Cupertino water

Cupertino is served by three separate water systems, split by neighbourhood: San Jose Water in the southeast, California Water Service in the northeast, and the City's own system in the west, which is itself operated by San Jose Water under a lease running to 2036 City of Cupertino. Which one applies to Property A cannot be determined without the address, which is not recorded.

San Jose Water's Schedule 1, effective July 1 2026, charges $80 a month just to have a 5/8-inch meter, then $5.0355 per ccf for the first 6 ccf, $7.5156 for 6–12, and $14.3548 above 12, plus a $3 monthly customer-assistance surcharge and a $0.2996/ccf surcharge San Jose Water Company (CPUC-jurisdictional tariff). A household using 10 ccf a month is at roughly $135–$155 for water alone, before any sewer charge. Against that, the supplied estimate of $100–$150 a month is plausible and possibly at the low end.

Property B — San Francisco water and sewer

SFPUC's FY2026-27 schedule charges $20 a month for a 5/8-inch meter, $12.03 per ccf for a multi-family account's first 3 units per dwelling unit and $12.91 beyond that, a $7 monthly wastewater service charge, $20.74 per discharge unit at a 95% flow factor for multi-family, and a Tier 3 stormwater charge of $30 a month for a parcel of 3,301–6,000 sq ft San Francisco Public Utilities Commission.

Working that through for three units at roughly 13 ccf a month produces about $330–$380 all-in — which lands inside the supplied $330–$400 range. So the reported water figure is consistent with the published tariff, which is worth saying explicitly because most of the supplied figures are not independently checkable.

Solar on Property A

The installation is stated to generate a credit of roughly $10–$20 a month. That is modelled as a $15 monthly credit. What is not known is the interconnection date, and that determines which tariff applies: the CPUC closed the net-energy-metering tariffs to new enrollments, and any system applying for interconnection on or after April 15 2023 is on the Net Billing Tariff, which compensates exports at the value to the grid rather than at retail rates California Public Utilities Commission.

Onsite consumption is offset either way. The surplus that produces the credit is what changes. Confirm the interconnection date before counting on it. Flagged F8.

What is missing entirely

Earthquake insurance. Standard California homeowners policies exclude it, no premium was supplied, and none can be verified at parcel level. It is carried at zero in the model, which means every projection here is slightly optimistic. For two pre-1980 Bay Area structures this is not a trivial omission.

Insurance: the weakest number in the model

Both properties are modelled at $1,500 a year. That is the figure supplied in the request, and it is an estimate — no official body publishes a parcel-level or even ZIP-level homeowner premium. The California Department of Insurance regulates rates and publishes approved filings; the rate-comparison tool is where a real quote comes from Unverified — blank

During research, several private quote-aggregation sites were found offering county-level "averages" for Santa Clara and San Francisco counties that disagreed with each other by a factor of three. None of those figures is used, shown, or cited here — they are marketing estimates, not official data, and a threefold spread is not a number worth planning on. They are recorded in the verification log only so it is clear they were seen and rejected.
