Decision Analysis

Three options, one comparable 10-year horizon, all numbers rounded to $10. The reasoning below each table explains why the recommendation follows.

Decision framework

Common to all three options: [BENEFICIARY] can live at Property B without losing its rental income (user-supplied, unverified), so housing has no incremental cost in this model. The federal estate tax is paid by the estate before distribution, so it is identical across options and cancels out of the comparison. The comparison is therefore about after-inheritance property economics. All three options are measured as net wealth after 10 years, assuming the property would be sold at the end of the horizon (with selling costs and federal gain tax) so that the sell option is not artificially advantaged.

Recommendation: Sell and reinvest

Sell Property A reasonably promptly after the trust transfer and reinvest the net proceeds across a diversified portfolio (equities plus a savings/Treasury allocation anchored to the CBO 4.3% forecast), while continuing to live at Property B and collect its rental income. The modelled 10-year advantage over renting is $1,939,330 in the 2045 central case. If the household wants to occupy Property A for non-financial reasons, confirm Prop 19 eligibility first: it is the only mechanism that materially reduces the tax, and it requires moving in within one year.

Before acting

Confirm (a) which property was the decedent's principal residence, (b) the actual tax rate area and special assessments from the tax bill, (c) real insurance quotes, (d) the solar/NEM interconnection date, and (e) California treatment of stepped-up basis with a licensed tax professional. These are all on the Flagged for Review page.
