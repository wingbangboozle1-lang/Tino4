Estate tax thresholds and headroom

How much can pass before tax at each level of government, how much room the house leaves for cash, and how that room is projected to change.

Short version: the house alone is far below the federal threshold. Whether any estate tax is due depends on the cash and other assets. The amount of cash is deliberately not shown here, so the tables give the room left after the house — compare it privately with the real figure.

How to use this with the cash amount

  1. Add the cash, investments, retirement accounts, life insurance and any other property the parents will own at death.
  2. Compare the total with the "Room for other assets" column for the expected year.
  3. If it is below the room: no federal estate tax, and no California tax in any case. Still file a return at the first parent's death to keep the unused exclusion.
  4. If it is above: every dollar over is taxed at 40%. Gifts within the yearly limit, and spending, reduce the total; the trust should be reviewed with an estate attorney.

Figures supplied by the owner are rounded to the nearest $10. Projections are estimates, not predictions.
