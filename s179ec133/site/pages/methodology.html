Method and privacy

How the numbers are produced, every parameter with its source, the checks that were run, and how personal information is kept out.

How the comparison works

  1. Start from the owner's figures, rounded to $10, and from rules read on official pages.
  2. Project each year from 2026 to 2076: prices, house values, assessed values (rising by inflation, at most 2%), tax bills (rate × assessed value + fixed charges), running costs and rents.
  3. At the inheritance year, split into three plans for Property A. Property B and the household's other income are the same in all three.
  4. Each year, work out income tax twice — with and without the plan's income — using the full federal and California bracket tables, the 3.8% investment-income tax and the California exemption for Treasury interest. The difference is the plan's tax.
  5. Put every spare dollar into, and take every shortfall out of, the same investment (stocks, a 60/40 mix, Treasuries or savings), so plans are compared on equal terms.
  6. At the end, turn everything into cash: sell the house (selling costs, tax on the gain since inheritance, the home-sale exclusion if lived in, depreciation recapture if rented) and sell the investments (tax on their gains).
  7. Repeat with real history in place of the steady rates.

What "verified" means here

  • The official page was opened by this session and the passage that supports the statement is quoted on Sources.
  • A figure the owner supplied is never called verified. It is labelled Owner-supplied and listed for review.
  • A figure the model computes is labelled Projection, and its row links to the official rule that drives it.
  • If a value could not be confirmed officially, it is left blank and flagged.

Privacy

  • No names, street addresses, parcel numbers, account numbers or ZIP codes appear anywhere in this dashboard or its working files.
  • Every figure supplied by the owner was rounded to the nearest $10 before it was written to any file. The model is run on the rounded figures, so nothing published can be worked back to an exact amount held in this project.
  • The amount of cash in the inheritance is not stored or shown; the estate tables are built so it is not needed.
  • Rounding lowers the chance of a search match; it is not a guarantee.
  • Pages carry a "noindex" tag asking search engines not to list them.

Limits

  • A model of this kind compares plans; it does not predict amounts. The ranking and the break-even points are the reliable part.
  • Tax rules are today's, carried forward with inflation. They will change.
  • Living in a home has value that is not money. The live-in plan's lower figure is its financial price, not a verdict on it.
  • This is general information, not legal, tax, investment or insurance advice.
