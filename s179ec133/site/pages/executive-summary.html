Executive summary

The answer first, then the facts behind it and the order of work.

1. Keep Property B. It pays about $85,800 a year in rent, its property tax is about $2,500 a year because its taxable value was set long ago, and the owners can live in the third unit. If it were reassessed today its tax would be about $22,390 a year. None of that survives a sale.

2. Sell Property A after inheriting it. Inheritance resets both its property-tax value (bad for keeping) and its income-tax basis (good for selling). A prompt sale turns about $5,435,870 (projected for 2045) into about $5,158,100 after selling costs, with little or no capital gains tax.

3. Invest the proceeds for growth. In a stock index fund the projected result after 20 years is $5,899,540 in today's dollars, against $3,729,430 for renting the house out and $2,736,710 for living in it.

4. If the family wants to live in Property A, that is affordable but costs about $3,162,830 of future wealth (today's dollars, 20 years) — and to keep the partial property-tax break a child must move in within one year and file two forms.

5. Renting Property A out is the weakest use of it. In 2046 the rent would be about $120,660, but property tax alone would take $65,190 of it.

What to do, in order

  1. Now: confirm the trust is a revocable living trust that holds title to Property A and that the parents' homeowners' exemption is on file. Steps and forms
  2. Now: add up the whole estate (house, cash, investments) and compare it with the table on Estate tax thresholds.
  3. At the transfer: get a written date-of-death appraisal, send the trustee notice within 60 days, and file the county change-in-ownership statement within 150 days. Deadlines
  4. Within about a year: sell Property A, or — if the family chooses to live there — move in and file the two county claims.
  5. Afterwards: invest the proceeds according to how much risk the household can carry; the comparison on Live, rent or sell shows the cost of each choice.

What could change the answer

  • A long stretch of poor stock returns (below about 3.8% a year) or very strong local house prices (above about 5.7% a year).
  • Keeping the proceeds in savings or Treasuries instead of investing for growth.
  • A change in the rules listed on Law changes to watch — especially the inherited-basis rule and the Proposition 19 rules.
  • The open items on Flagged for Review: the trust type, the household's tax bracket, insurance quotes and the true rent Property A would earn.
