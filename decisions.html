CALIFORNIA PROPERTY RESEARCH BRIEF · 2026
04 / DECISION ANALYSIS

Three paths.
One honest comparison.

The best answer depends on where the heir will live, qualifying ownership, verified costs and after-tax returns. An unsupported break-even rent or investment return would be misleading.

OptionEconomic testPrincipal uncertaintySource
Live in ACompare avoided alternative housing cost and any eligible property-tax saving against ownership costs and foregone after-tax investment returns.Prop 19 eligibility and partial reassessment, insurance, repairs, opportunity cost.BOE ↗
Rent A; live elsewhereAfter-tax rent = collected rent − vacancy − operating costs − property tax − income tax; compare with the after-tax return on net sale proceeds.Rent, vacancy, insurance, maintenance, income tax, rental law and future assessments are unverified.FTB ↗ · CA law ↗
Sell A and investInvestable proceeds = sale price − selling costs − debt − tax. Compare risk-adjusted after-tax future returns and the cost of replacement housing.Sale price, basis, gains, costs, investment return and state tax are unknown.IRS basis ↗
Keep B as a rental / homeInclude B in every option on the same basis. Living in one unit does not by itself establish the treatment of all units or ownership interests.Existing rent, occupancy, assessment, legal unit status and tax allocation are unverified.BOE ↗ · SF Rent Board ↗
Decision rule. Prefer the option with the higher risk-adjusted, after-tax net worth and usable cash flow over the same period, including housing services and the same treatment of the second property. No reliable numeric ranking is currently possible. A sale does not automatically create zero taxable gain; future value, selling costs, estate inclusion and basis require verification.

Private calculations to perform

For a common horizon, calculate annual cash flow and ending after-tax assets for each option, including appreciation, rental income, vacancies, maintenance, insurance, utilities, tax reassessments, mortgage/debt, selling expenses, income/capital gains tax and investment gains. Avoid double-counting the San Francisco rental income, and value the occupied unit’s housing benefit consistently. No rate of return is guaranteed. IRS estate guidance ↗
