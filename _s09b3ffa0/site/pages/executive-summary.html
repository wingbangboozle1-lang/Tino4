Core Analytical Findings & Structural Asymmetry

Three California and federal statutory rules interact at the moment of inheritance to create a decisive financial asymmetry between [Property A] and [Property B]:

  • Tax-Free Exit Window at Death (IRC §1014 & CA RTC §18031): Assuming [Property A] is held in a standard revocable living trust included in the settlor's gross estate (Rev. Rul. 2023-2), its income-tax basis resets to 100% of date-of-death fair market value (projected $6,296,680 nominal / $4,012,470 real 2026$ in 2045). Selling shortly after death incurs $0 federal LTCG, $0 3.8% NIIT, and $0 California 13.3% income tax on decades of prior appreciation — only brokerage/closing friction (~5.0%) plus the verified 0.11% county documentary transfer tax.
  • Proposition 19 Reassessment Penalty on Holding [Property A] (CA RTC §63.2): Unlike income-tax basis, California's Proposition 13 property-tax base does not carry over intact. Renting [Property A] triggers 100% reassessment to market value ($75,370/yr nominal Year-1 property tax vs. $10,340/yr before death). Even moving into [Property A] within 1 year only shields FBYV + indexed $1,044,590 [$10-rounded] cap, leaving a $51,150/yr nominal Year-1 tax bill.
  • Locked-In Proposition 13 Advantage on Already-Owned [Property B]: Because [Property B] is already owned by the inheritor, no change in ownership occurs. Its 2025 net assessed value is just $114,250 ($2,440/yr tax on a $1,800,000 building), while two rented units already produce $7,150/mo ($85,800/yr) and Unit 3 provides primary housing at zero incremental housing outlay.
