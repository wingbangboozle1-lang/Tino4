Decision overview

A planning framework for an anticipated trust transfer and a separate already-owned property. Owner-supplied figures are published privacy-rounded to the nearest ten dollars; no names, addresses or parcel numbers appear anywhere.

Conditional best plan under the baseline assumptions (owner-supplied, privacy-rounded figures)

Live in Property A after the transfer and claim the family-home exclusion: occupy within one year, file the homeowners' exemption within one year, and file the intergenerational claim within three years. At the modeled 2045 transfer that keeps the value limit — not the whole long-held base — and costs about $62,040 per year in property tax, versus about $87,250 per year if the home is instead reassessed — a difference of about $25,200 every year, about $275,960 over the first ten years. Keep Property B as is: its rental income continues under either choice. Selling A instead nets about $6,074,050 after assumed sale costs and moves the decision from property tax to reinvestment risk. REINVESTMENT RISK, BESIDE THE HEADLINE: any sale proceeds parked in 3-month-to-2-year Treasuries for 24-36 months earn the then-current auction rate, which may be lower at each renewal; staged buying also lags a rising market. See the black-swan page before treating a sale as the default.

Reinvestment risk sits beside the headline

Parking sale proceeds in 3-month-to-2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months changes when the money enters the market, not how risky the market is. Each maturing rung is reinvested at an unknown future auction rate, a rising market makes staged buying lag, and a decline that begins after staging ends hits a fully invested balance. The local-only ladder panel quantifies the first effect.

Keep the ownership roles distinct

Property A is the anticipated trust transfer. Property B is already owned. A choice to occupy, rent or sell one asset must not silently assume that the other asset has the same owner, tax basis, assessment, lease or operating costs.

Use this workspace in order

1. Resolve the questions

Collect the trust, tax, title, lease, assessment, insurance and utility records in a private vault.

2. Compare complete paths

Rounded-figure policy (owner-directed)

At the owner's written instruction, the figures that drive this decision are published, rounded to the nearest ten dollars so they cannot be searched back to a source record: 'Costs and numbers should be used but rounded to the nearest $10 increments ... and should be reported in any decision analysis.' Owner-supplied rows are badged Owner-supplied (privacy-rounded) and linked to the official record that can confirm them; projections are badged Projection or Assumption; values that are simply unknown stay blank. Names, street addresses and parcel identifiers are never published.

Planning framework only. Not legal, tax, insurance, appraisal or investment advice. A source supports only its stated scope, not a private fact. Owner-supplied figures are privacy-rounded to the nearest $10 at the owner's direction and flagged; blanks mean unknown, not zero.
