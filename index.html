Decision overview

A privacy-first decision framework: rounded household figures, official rules, and what would change the answer.

Rounded-figure disclosure (owner directive, 2026-11-17)

Household costs and values on this site are published rounded to the nearest $10, marked 'Household-reported (rounded)', because the owner requires the decision analysis to report its numbers. Properties appear only as Property A (the home being inherited through a trust) and Property B (the already-owned three-unit home). Names, street addresses, postal codes, parcel identifiers and property locality names are never published, and unrounded source values stay in the private workpaper. Rounding is a disclosure choice, not anonymization: see the Privacy page for what it does and does not protect.

Read this page in plain language

Three things are true at once, and keeping them apart is the whole point of this site. First, the inheritance is not one decision; it is a sequence of dated decisions, and the earliest ones are the hardest to undo. Second, several of those decisions turn on facts that are not yet written down anywhere: whether someone will actually live in the home, what it is worth on the day it transfers, and what the local transfer tax is. Third, a projection that fills those gaps with guesses will still produce a confident-looking ranking, and that ranking will be an artefact of the guesses.

So each table below is organised the same way. The fact is what the decision depends on. The current state says whether that fact is established, missing, or a labeled assumption built from the rounded household inputs. The evidence column says who can supply it and in what form. A row marked Missing is a task, not a zero.

The practical order is: settle the occupancy question, obtain dated valuations and written quotes, read the local schedules, then compare the paths on identical dates and one horizon. The break-even page reduces the biggest unknown to a single figure you can go and get, and the executive summary states the conditional leading plan at current inputs.

Safe decision order

Set the family's written decision rules before seeing a forecast. Confirm trust and title, get independent values, establish tax basis and assessed-value treatment, validate use and leases, price recurring costs, then compare every path at the same dates and horizon. Keep all exact inputs in a locally controlled, non-public workpaper.

Conditional fork

If a qualifying heir intends to occupy the transferred home as a principal residence long-term, test the Proposition 19 family-home exclusion and its deadlines. If not, model the reassessed-tax branch and compare rental, sale, or mixed paths. No branch can be chosen from public facts alone.

Where the numbers come from

Every statutory amount on this site carries the date it was read from the issuing authority and the words that establish it; see the Verified lookups page. Household amounts are rounded to $10 and labeled. Amounts that could not be confirmed are left blank and flagged rather than estimated. Re-reading the official sources is the first task of the annual review, because an amount that was correct this year is not evidence about a transfer that may happen decades from now.

Research framework only; not legal, tax, insurance, appraisal, investment, or property-management advice.

Recheck official authorities, source availability and law at the actual decision date.
