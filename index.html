Overview

A decision guide for a home that will be inherited through a trust between 2040 and 2050, kept side by side with a rental building the inheritor already owns. Every figure links to the official source it rests on.

Best plan on the numbers: keep Property B exactly as it is, and when Property A is inherited, sell it within about a year and invest what is left after costs in a low-cost stock index fund (or a stock-heavy mix).

Why: inheriting resets Property A's taxable value, so its yearly property tax would rise from about $10,850 to about $49,880 (living in it) or $65,150 (renting it out) in 2045. The same event resets its tax cost basis to market value, so selling soon after triggers little or no capital gains tax. Property B is the opposite: its low taxable value and its rents are worth keeping, and selling it would be taxed.

How firm is this: selling wins in the baseline for every inheritance year from 2040 to 2050, and in 96% of the 49 replays of real 1977–2025 history. It stops winning if stocks return less than about 3.8% a year for 20 years, or if the money would sit in savings or Treasuries while local house prices repeat their long-run record. See Black-swan conditions.
