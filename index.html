TWO HOMES, ONE INHERITANCE, ONE DECISION

Should you live in it,
rent it, or sell it?

Property A is a single-family home to be inherited through a trust between 2040 and 2050. Property B is a three-unit building you already own and partly rent. Every figure below is rounded to the nearest ten dollars before it is used, every rule is linked to the official page that states it, and every gap is left blank.

Read the executive summary ↗
THE ANSWER, STATED PLAINLY

Live in it,
or sell it.
Do not rent it.

On these rounded inputs, renting Property A after transfer loses money every year for two decades while a sell-and-reinvest path compounds. Renting only wins if rent is far higher than assumed, and that case fails on a rent-to-price ratio the local rent caps make hard to reach.

The one comparison that decides this

If the trust distributes in 2045 and A is worth $6,531,230 on that date, selling nets $6,132,170 after selling costs and the county transfer tax. Left invested for twenty years at the assumptions on the methods page it is worth $14,568,100 after tax. Renting for the same twenty years and then selling is worth $10,977,570 after tax, because the running costs and the reassessment at transfer exceed the rent. That gap is the whole decision.
