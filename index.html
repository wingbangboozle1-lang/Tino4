Decision overview

A private planning framework for an anticipated trust transfer and a separate already-owned property. Case figures are rounded to the nearest $10; no addresses, postal codes or identifiers are published.

Working recommendation (conditional): keep Property B, sell Property A near the transfer, stage the proceeds

On the verified rules and owner-supplied figures, the default path is: keep and continue operating the already-owned San Francisco property; at the trust transfer, sell the inherited house soon after the date of death so the stepped-up basis keeps income tax minimal; park net proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months and phase into equities. Reinvestment risk sits beside this headline: maturing bills may renew at lower yields, and phasing only softens entry risk when a crash occurs during the wait — see the worked example. The alternative — move into the inherited house and claim the family-home exclusion — is quantified above and wins only if the household actually wants that residence. All of this stays conditional on the gates below.

Keep the ownership roles distinct

Property A is the anticipated trust transfer. Property B is already owned. A choice to occupy, rent or sell one asset must not silently assume that the other asset has the same owner, tax basis, assessment, lease or operating costs.

Use this workspace in order

1. Resolve the questions

Collect the trust, tax, title, lease, assessment, insurance and utility records in a private vault.

2. Compare complete paths

Keep live, rent, sell, mixed occupancy and staged reinvestment alternatives separate.

3. Review with advisers

Print a one-page attorney and CPA briefing sheet; no identifying details are required on it.
