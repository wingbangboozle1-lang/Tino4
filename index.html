Decision overview

A privacy-first decision framework: official rules and questions are public; household facts stay private.

No household-specific recommendation is published

The public repository is not a safe place for private property, tax, trust, lease, or investment data. This overview therefore gives decision gates and official rules, but does not rank a household path or expose identifying figures.

Urgent privacy notice: historical exposure is unresolved

The current site is generalized, but prior commits, pull-request views, forks, caches, downloads, or clones may retain older public material. This cleanup does not prove that historical copies are erased. Repository administrators must assess and coordinate any history remediation; do not upload private case records here. Read this page in plain language

Three things are true at once, and keeping them apart is the whole point of this site. First, the inheritance is not one decision; it is a sequence of dated decisions, and the earliest ones are the hardest to undo. Second, several of those decisions turn on facts that are not yet written down anywhere: whether someone will actually live in the home, what it is worth on the day it transfers, and what the local transfer tax is. Third, a projection that fills those gaps with guesses will still produce a confident-looking ranking, and that ranking will be an artefact of the guesses.

So each table below is organised the same way. The fact is what the decision depends on. The current state says whether that fact is established, missing, or merely a rule. The evidence column says who can supply it and in what form. A row marked Missing is a task, not a zero: nothing on this site substitutes a placeholder for an unknown number.

The practical order is: settle the occupancy question, obtain dated valuations and written quotes, read the local schedules, then compare the six paths on identical dates and one horizon. The break-even page reduces the biggest unknown to a single figure you can go and get.

Safe decision order

Set the family's written decision rules before seeing a forecast. Confirm trust and title, get independent values, establish tax basis and assessed-value treatment, validate use and leases, price recurring costs, then compare every path at the same dates and horizon. Keep all private inputs in a locally controlled, non-public workpaper.

Conditional fork

If a qualifying heir intends to occupy the transferred home as a principal residence long-term, test the Proposition 19 family-home exclusion and its deadlines. If not, model the reassessed tax branch and compare rental, sale, or mixed paths. No branch can be chosen from public facts alone.

Research framework only; not legal, tax, insurance, appraisal, investment, or property-management advice.

Recheck official authorities, source availability and law at the actual decision date.
